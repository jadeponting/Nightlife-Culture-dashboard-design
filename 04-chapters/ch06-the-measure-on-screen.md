# Chapter 6 — The measure on screen

*Dashboard Design · Part 1: The display*
*Draft for steer · 17 August 2026*

---

## The decision

**When a measure reaches the screen, what does its form commit you to — and what does the screen do back to the measure?**

---

## The 60-second version

> Whether the Nightlife Cultural Value Index should be an index at all is a decision the house
> has deliberately kept open: *"the result may be an index, a diagnostic, a framework, a value
> ledger, a modular evidence toolkit or a hybrid — or a decision that a scored index is not
> useful."* This chapter does not close that question. It establishes what each form **does on a
> screen**, because the screen is not a neutral carrier: a composite displayed becomes a rank;
> a dial implies a target; a colour scale implies a judgement; a ledger implies two sides. The
> form decision should be made knowing what each display will do with it.
>
> The evidence is blunt about composites. Every aggregation rule embeds exchange rates —
> under simple addition, a profile of (21,1,1,1) equals (6,6,6,6) — and published weights are
> routinely cosmetic: in one famous ranking, an indicator nominally worth 20% drove 21% of the
> variance while another worth 40% drove 81%. The incumbent instrument in this sector publishes
> three part-scores and a headline that is not the mean of its own parts, with the weights
> undisclosed. So the one Fixed rule that holds whatever form is chosen: **the recipe on the
> face.** A measure whose construction cannot be shown simply enough to survive the phone test
> is not simplified by hiding the recipe — it is laundered.
>
> And two house steers become measure-design questions here: the **attribution flip** ("x% of
> overnight stays began with an event we put on") and the **dynamic census** (economic impact as
> a living series, not a clipboard night). Both are displays of a claim as much as of a number,
> and both are survivable by a hostile reader only if the claim's boundary is drawn on screen.

## How the decision is made

### Step one — know what each form commits you to on screen

| Form | What it is on screen | What the display commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **Composite index** | One number per place | The crop-ready rank (Ch 5's whole problem); implicit exchange rates displayed as if none exist; uncertainty intervals on any ordering; and an audit apparatus that needs many units — at small N the score is undefendable |
| **Profile / indicator set** | A shape, not a number | Small-multiples discipline; no tiebreak between places (which is honest); no single headline — the form's cost is that it cannot be shouted |
| **Diagnostic** | Conditions against thresholds | "What would help" as the reading; comparison against *standards*, not against other places; policy-actionable by construction; needs defensible thresholds |
| **Value ledger** | Two-sided accounting: created versus extracted | Flow displays; visible double-entry honesty (what comes in *and* what leaves); the attribution question lives here — and it is the form that matches the studio's founding sentence: *they create value, the institutions extract it* |
| **Boundary / doughnut** | Health within limits | Non-compensatory by construction — nothing on one side of the ring buys back an overshoot on the other; no ranking is even expressible; the strongest glance-form of the five; commits you to defining the limits |

None of these is recommended here. The table exists so the form decision — which belongs to the
methodology work and to Carly — is made with the display consequences priced in. What the
research does license: **one canonical data layer can drive several expressions.** Identity
wants a profile, conditions want a diagnostic, value flows want a ledger. The screen does not
force a premature marriage; it forces each expression to obey its own commitments.

### Step two — the recipe on the face

The aggregation literature's findings, graduated from the raw research and load-bearing here:

- **Every aggregation rule embeds exchange rates.** Under linear aggregation a deficit in one
  dimension is offset by a surplus in another at a constant rate — the worked illustration is
  that **(21,1,1,1) and (6,6,6,6) are identical**. Geometric aggregation punishes imbalance;
  non-compensatory methods refuse offsetting entirely. **[Evaluated — the standard handbook
  literature; agent-fetched.]** Translated: under addition, a corporate late-night drinking
  strip with a collapsed grassroots scene can equal a modest broad ecology. Choosing the rule
  *is* the claim that those are substitutable.
- **Published weights are routinely not the real weights.** Sensitivity analysis on flagship
  indices found nominal weights "little more than cosmetic": one ranking's indicator at 20%
  nominal weight explained 21% of variance while a 40% indicator explained 81%. The real
  weights come from correlation structure nobody negotiated. **[Measured; agent-fetched.]**
- **The incumbent fails exactly here.** Creative Footprint's Rotterdam report publishes part
  scores of 8.58, 5.90 and 5.41 and a headline of **6.10** — when the mean of its own parts is
  6.63 — with no disclosed weighting anywhere in its methodology. **[Agent-fetched; the
  arithmetic is checkable from its own published numbers.]** A published composite that cannot
  be reproduced from its published components is the display failure this chapter exists to
  prevent.

So the Fixed rule, whatever form wins: **the recipe on the face.** If the measure is composed,
the composition is displayed — what went in, at what weight, with the implicit exchange rate
stated in real units. The refusal test from the raw research is the right one and survives
verbatim: *how many new venues does the measure say compensate for the loss of one
forty-year-old institution?* If the display cannot answer that question, the measure is not
ready for a screen. And the house guardrail sits underneath as the boundary condition: *"Where
would scoring create false precision or harmful incentives?"* **[Axiom.]**

### Step three — pay for "scene intensity"

Chapter 5 used the term as a placeholder; here is what the display requires of it, leaving its
indicator construction to the methodology work, which governs.

Scene intensity is an **intensity**, not a volume: participation, belonging and activity *per
head or per participant*, built from Chapter 4's registers (the placed-mark distributions, the
counted cues, event frequency normalised by community size — not by borough population, which
would re-import the density trap). Its display commitments:

- **Denominator on the face** — the defect readers are least able to catch unaided (Chapter 1's
  literacy evidence), and the entire content of the Hull-outscores-London claim. If the
  denominator is hidden, the reordering reads as an error; shown, it reads as the finding.
- **Registers stay visible.** Where a component is self-selected (the playable survey), the
  provenance says so on the same screen — intensity built partly from self-placed marks is
  legitimate *and labelled*, never laundered into an objective-looking number.
- **It never merges with conditions.** Intensity is the identity side's summary statistic;
  support, space and tenure are the conditions side. One number across both is the forbidden
  composite arriving through the back door.

### Step four — the attribution flip, displayed so it survives

The measure: **origination** — visits whose observable first cause was a ticket. The politics:
*"we attracted them here in the first place — you got that metric because of us"*; the levy
share; the destination-management case. **[House steer.]** The display problem: "because of us"
is precisely where a hostile reader — an economist, a rival sector, a sceptical council — will
strike, and the counter is always the same word: *correlation*.

**And the mechanics are not speculative — they are already in the sector's hands.** Every
ticket carries a postcode and a purchase timestamp. **[House steer, 17 August 2026.]** A buyer
living over a travel-time threshold implies an overnight stay; an event ending after the last
train implies a taxi or a bed; destination management organisations hold hotel capacity data,
and hotel price spikes already tell this story for football and for Pride — nobody disputes
that Manchester hotels surge when United play at home. Almost every promoter can already tell
you where their people came from and when they bought. The city-side data exists; **the work is
linking, anonymising and governing it, not inventing it.**

The display answer is a **claim ladder drawn on screen**. The strong claim — *the ticket was
bought before the trip; the event was the visit's anchor* — is displayed with its base, its
anonymisation method, and its boundary: what it does **not** assert (that the event caused the
hotel choice, the restaurant spend, the second night). The weaker associational claims sit
visibly below it. The flipped sentence — *"x% of overnight stays began with an event"* — is a
breakout statistic, and Chapter 6 of the Decision Book already legislates for it: it travels
further than any chart, so it carries its source line, its base and its method *in the
statistic*, because it will be screenshotted alone. The form that fits the fuller story is the
**flow display** (Chapter 3's step zero family): source → visit → stay → spend, with the
ledger's honesty — showing also what the night *didn't* originate. An attribution display that
only ever flows one way is advocacy; one that shows its counter-flows is evidence.

### Step five — the measure as a living series

The dynamic census reframes the measure's relationship with time, and time is a display
dimension with its own commitments:

- **A snapshot presented as a state is the clipboard census's ghost.** The one-night count's
  deepest flaw was not labour cost but *stillness*. The living measure displays as a series —
  with its seasonality honest, because nightlife breathes annually and a January reading
  presented bare is a lie about the year.
- **The leading-indicator claim is a two-track display.** *"Nightlife creates dynamism before
  it registers in the data"* **[house steer]** is a claim about lag: the scene moves first, the
  administrative statistics move later. The display that makes this arguable rather than
  asserted is two time-tracks on one screen — the ticketing-led series running ahead, the
  official series (employment, business counts) trailing — with the lag visible and, over time,
  testable. This is also the university story's display: the vibe series leading, the
  enrolment and rent series following.
- **Freshness is part of the measure.** A living series that silently stops living is the
  observatory again. The series displays its own last heartbeat — Chapter 8 inherits this.

### Step six — the joy metric runs alongside, not instead

The standard economic development metrics are made by administrations and map what they care
about; the night barely features. **[House steer.]** The scholarly version of the same
conclusion comes from the sector's own foundational review: cultural value resists a single
metric and is better held as *"a framework within which sits a variety of components… each of
them to be evaluated by appropriate and often different methodologies."* **[Evaluated — the
Arts and Humanities Research Council's Cultural Value Project; agent-fetched.]**

The display consequence closes the loop with Chapter 2: the measure — whatever form it takes —
is designed to sit **beside** deprivation indices, job density and travel-to-work times, in the
rooms where those are already on screen. Which means: it states its units and vintages to their
standards (the comparability gate), it survives their visual conventions without adopting their
judgement ramps, and it says plainly what it adds that they structurally omit. Not a rival
statistics office. The missing column, installed.

## Worked example

*Illustrative — one place's measure screen; fixes nothing, and deliberately shows the plural
expression rather than a chosen form.*

**Top — the profile (identity):** the sonic fingerprint and the intensity figure, denominator
visible: *"per 1,000 scene participants"* — with the self-placed components labelled. No rank
anywhere.

**Middle — the diagnostic (conditions):** space, support, tenure against stated thresholds —
each threshold's source named — reading as *what would help*, not *who is winning*.

**The ledger strip (flows):** value created and value retained, side by side, including the
origination statistic in full breakout discipline: *"31% of out-of-area attendees stayed
overnight — ticket bought a median 12 days before travel · anonymised ticketing, n stated ·
claims the visit's anchor, not the whole stay's spend."* Beside it, the counter-flow honestly:
what the night imports as well as what it attracts.

**Bottom — the living series:** the ticketing-led track running ahead of the official
employment track, lag visible, seasonality shown as a shaded annual rhythm, and the series'
last heartbeat stamped — *data to Thursday*.

**The five-second test:** a reader should say *"this place's scene is real, under-backed, and
pulling people in — and I can see how they know."* The "how they know" clause is the recipe on
the face, working.

## Pressure points

**Where there is real latitude.**

*The ledger as the signature form.* Value created versus value extracted is the studio's
founding sentence, and no incumbent instrument displays it. If any form becomes this
instrument's recognisable shape, the two-sided ledger is the candidate with the deepest house
roots — and it is the form the attribution flip naturally lives in.

*The boundary form for ecosystem health.* A nightlife doughnut — the ecology healthy within
limits, overshoot and shortfall non-compensable by construction — is the strongest available
glance-form for "an ecosystem has the tallest trees, the grassroots and the mycelium," and
worth a probe.

*Naming.* "Scene intensity," "origination," "the joy metric" — the house names measures better
than the field does, and a well-named measure carries its meaning where no methodology note
follows.

**Where it looks rigorous and fails.**

*The dial.* A speedometer implies a target and a judgement; nightlife has neither a speed limit
nor a finish line.

*The judgement ramp.* Score bands coloured very-low-to-very-high against a national average
re-imports the deficit frame and the league table in one artefact — the ink-steps rule exists
precisely here.

*The undisclosed-weights composite.* The incumbent's failure, and after this chapter, a known
one: publishing a headline that cannot be recomputed from its published parts.

*The index of everything.* Folding identity, conditions, flows and feelings into one number —
every prior chapter's forbidden move at once, with the exchange rates hidden.

*The permanent pilot.* A "dynamic" series shown with three data points and a trend line.
Chapter 8's uncertainty rules apply to time too.

**What proves a break works.** Hand the screen to an economist with the instruction "break the
headline number." If everything they need to interrogate it — recipe, base, boundary,
counter-flow, lag — is already on the screen, the measure survives its first committee room.
Then hand it to a promoter: if the number still smells like their night, it survives the
dancefloor. A measure that passes only one of those two tests is not finished.

## Checklist

- [ ] No form chosen by default; display consequences priced for whichever is proposed
- [ ] Recipe on the face of anything composed; headline recomputable from displayed parts
- [ ] The exchange-rate question answerable from the screen
- [ ] Scene intensity carries its denominator; self-selected components labelled; never merged with conditions
- [ ] Origination displayed with claim ladder, boundary and counter-flows
- [ ] The breakout statistic self-contained: base, method, source within it
- [ ] The measure displays as a living series with honest seasonality and a visible heartbeat
- [ ] Leading-indicator claims shown as two tracks with the lag visible
- [ ] Designed to sit beside administrative metrics at their comparability standards
- [ ] The economist test and the promoter test both run
