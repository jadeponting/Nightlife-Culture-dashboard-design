## The decision

**Is this a rule with a right answer or a defensible range — and when you deviate, can you say what it bought and prove it held?**

## The 60-second version

> The Decision Book's closing machinery transfers whole, and is not re-derived: the tier test
> (Fixed is uncertainty — there is a right answer; Adaptable is ambiguity — defensible or
> not; Decorative is free); the four kinds of deviation (**ignorant, lazy, expedient,
> earned** — and only the record separates expedient from earned); the four-line override log;
> and the rule that a system's gaps carry interim positions or get filled at 11pm by whoever
> has the file open.
>
> What this chapter adds is this book's own: **the assembled removal tests** — every chapter's
> "what proves a break works" in one table, because almost every test turns out to be a form
> of removal or of adversarial walking: take away the interaction, the colour, the sound, the
> goodwill, the large numbers — and see what is left standing. Plus the seeded **rule
> register** for the build, and the honest **gap summary**: what this research could not find,
> and the seven places where the instrument would be doing something with no precedent at
> all — which is either the risk list or the contribution list, and is in fact both.

## The assembled removal tests

*Use one row, once, at the moment of deviation — not the table at once.*

| Ch | The break contemplated | What proves it works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | An unconventional default view | The five-second test: shown cold, the intended visitor speaks the sentence you wrote — and everything crucial survives with no click |
| 2 | An unconventional two-columns layout | The five-second test on a *harm-frame holder*: they state the claim accurately, without hostility; no shared axis appeared |
| 3 | A map where the rules say maybe-not, or a non-geographic map | State what a mark is and the denominator, aloud; the small-scene member finds their scene, or sees *uncounted* — never *absent* |
| 4 | An expressive felt-layer display | The represented-not-used test, run with a contributor; the precise version stands alone |
| 5 | Any ordered view at all | The crop test — screenshot any region; what travels carries uncertainty and gate. And the losers' test with a low-intensity place |
| 6 | A composed measure | The economist test ("break this number" — everything needed is on screen) and the promoter test (it still smells like their night) |
| 7 | A discovery surface | The kid test, the scene-consent reversibility test, and the Form 696 test: enforcement opens it tomorrow and gains nothing unchosen |
| 8 | Any new state or filter | The adversary's walk to a small scene; the staging-decay week (can the instrument look stale?) |
| 9 | A new interaction | Name its row; static reading works without it; one cold user either uses it or is the 85% |
| 10 | An unconventional story shape | The two-readers test: story-only and exploration-only readers reach the same finding |
| 11 | Any access trade | Unplug the mouse, zoom 400%, then the blind-fan test — the answer matches the sighted reader's |
| 12 | Adopting a gallery pattern | The entry's own "why it holds" conditions, checked against this instrument's claim |
| 13 | An expressive register move | Mono, silent, still, undressed — the evidence complete in what remains; both audiences tested, neither paying for the other |

The pattern the Decision Book found holds here: **work that survives removal was built on
structure.** The additions this book made to the family are the adversarial walks — the crop,
the small scene, the enforcement reader — because an interactive instrument's breaks are
reachable *states*, and states are tested by walking to them with bad intent.

## The deviation record

Inherited verbatim **[axiom]**: every deviation logs four lines — *trigger · rule and tier ·
alternative chosen · evidence (which test above, and what it showed)* — kept with the build in
`07-checks/overrides.md`. Chapter 9's interaction budget and Chapter 13's expressive palette
both write into this log by construction. An unlogged expedient deviation is
indistinguishable from negligence at the next audit, including to us.

## The rule register, seeded

`07-checks/rule-register.md` is seeded from this book: **circa 150 Fixed-tier rules across
Chapters 1–13**, one line each with source chapter, plus the Adaptable set with their
latitude named. Three build-time checks are specified for the eventual `07-checks/` scripts,
in the Decision Book's enforcement spirit (a rule that can be measured is a rule that is
run): the **crop check** (no exportable state renders an ordered all-places list), the
**recipe check** (every displayed composite recomputes from its displayed parts), and the
**state-honesty check** (every reachable state carries heartbeat, gaps-with-reasons, and
threshold behaviour). The full register and scripts are the build sprint's first task —
seeded now, enforced then.

## The gap register, summarised

Across thirteen chapters, the four-field gap log accumulated into two lists.

**What the field could not supply** (the honest holes, each carried in its chapter): no
published control-usage analytics for any statistical interactive; overview-first never
validated; the empty-state cost unquantified; no map-versus-non-map task experiments; no
display research on cultural asset maps; dominance-display readability untested; uncertainty
display untested *under filtering*; scrollytelling unevaluated for comprehension;
recommendation explanations never studied in a place domain; recorded-audio-as-display
unevaluated for blind users; the seasonality display of rhythmic annual data unresearched.
**The probe programme in `03-tests/` is, in aggregate, a research agenda the field itself is
missing — the instrument can publish evidence, not just use it.**

**Where this instrument would be first** (the original-contribution candidates, each specified
in its chapter, none yet tested): the **"what this changed" provenance field** (Ch 4); the
**money-map-over-scene-map** underinvestment overlay (Ch 7); **community-level control over
being recommended** (Ch 7); the **visibly-ageing instrument** (Ch 8); the
**identity/conditions hard split** with the crop test (Ch 5); the **origination claim
ladder** (Ch 6); the **guess-as-perception-data** moment (Ch 9); the **"how this instrument
argues" page** (Ch 10); the **committee-export-at-lower-volume** (Ch 13). Nine candidates.
If even half survive testing, the instrument is not a better dashboard — it is a new kind of
thing, which was the brief's opening sentence.

## What remains, awaiting steer

one. The book is drafted; nothing in it is finished until it has survived you.

## Checklist — for any future deviation

- [ ] Tier established before the argument started
- [ ] The relevant removal test row identified *before* committing
- [ ] The test run, and passed, on the real artefact
- [ ] The four-line override logged in `07-checks/overrides.md`
- [ ] If the system had no rule: the gap logged in four fields, with an interim position
- [ ] A closed gap names the rule that would have caught it
