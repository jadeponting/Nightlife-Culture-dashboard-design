# Library pass — the sources the book could not read in full

*Run 7 October 2026 (research log, Entry 17), at Carly's instruction, on two lists: the dagger-marked sources behind the 51 Fixed-tiered rules that rest on an abstract or a secondary account (`06-register/work/dagger-fixed-rows.csv`), and the named texts in the chapters' gap tables that no sprint could reach. Two agents, thirty searches each, WebFetch only; every quotation marked STRING-VERIFIED (a second fetch with a YES/NO presence check and a false control string) or SINGLE PASS; a verification pass re-traced twelve of the changed readings and found the load-bearing strings present, with five details corrected below. Nothing in the chapters has been rewritten: by the book's method, a claim found to need changing is logged here and flagged for Carly, and the chapter changes when she says so. The register (`06-register/rule-register.csv`) carries the dagger status on every rule that rests on one of these sources.*

## What the pass found, in one page

Ninety-six distinct sources were attempted (44 behind the Chapter 1–9 rows; 18 behind the Chapter 10–16 rows; 34 named texts). Thirty-four were read in full or in the part that matters — an open copy found and opened; twenty-one more are open but behind a wall the fetch tool cannot pass (PubMed Central's challenge page; robots rules; the tool's page cap) and need a browser, not a library; forty-one have no open copy and carry a citation for a library request. Of the thirty-four read, eleven simply support the chapter as written, and twenty-three change something: nine of substance, the rest a caveat or a number.

**Corrections proposed, for Carly to approve before any chapter changes** (each is traced in the tables below, with the exact sentence and where it sits):

1. *Chapter 7, rule 7.2 (no league table and no bands), and the RSS working party of 2005.* The report does not forbid league tables or bands; it says "League tables without demonstration of ranking uncertainty should be avoided and, even with uncertainty incorporated, such tables should be used with considerable caution" and that banding "must incorporate adequate allowance for uncertainty". It supports the register's interval rule (any ordering carries its interval), not the prohibition, which stands on the house's own grounds and the *Doing Business* review. The rule's source cell should say so.
2. *Chapter 1, rule 1.7 and its text ("the clearest demonstration in any field"), and Kaliisa et al. 2024.* The proxy-moves-before-the-goal finding rests on nine of 38 studies, five with medium or large effects against four small or negligible (the split single pass), and the authors say the larger effects were, in almost all cases, confounded with another intervention or uncontrolled. The sentence overstates the review; the same paragraph prints "lived up the promise" where the preprint has "lived up to the promise".
3. *Chapter 9, rule 9.17 ("what this changed"; Fix My Street's 57 per cent), and Sjoberg, Mellon & Peixoto 2017.* The 399,364 are reports from 154,957 people; "responsiveness" is a first report marked fixed within 35 days by another user, unverified; the 57 per cent is 11.6 against 18.2 per cent; "we cannot estimate causal effects per se". The study is about a problem being answered by action, not about contributors being told what changed.
4. *Chapter 11, rule 11.11 (linking in a slideshow layout), and Zhi, Ottley & Metoyer 2019.* The why-cell says linking "improved recall"; the paper says "We did not find any evidence for linking improving the recall task performance" and no difference on comprehension; linking moved self-reported engagement, time and interactions; 167 analysed, one story. The printed means also run opposite to the paper's stated direction under its own score definition, which needs a human look at Figures 5 and 6.
5. *Chapter 16, rule 16.4 and truth 2, and Paterno et al. 2009.* "The severe rules are obeyed where set apart" rests on a tier that could not be disobeyed ("by design since this was a 'hard stop'"); the free-choice evidence for tiering is the middle tier's 29 against 10 per cent. Chapter 16 §6 already carries the caveat; truth 2 and the rule should carry it too.
6. *Chapter 12, rule 12.26, and Tintarev & Masthoff 2012.* Effectiveness is supported ("personalization was in most cases clearly detrimental to effectiveness"); "raised satisfaction" is the abstract's "may improve", and in the experiment where people tried the items the satisfaction gain was absent. The rule stands; one half of its cell overstates.
7. *Chapters 2 and 9 (rows 2.23, 9.33), and Birt et al. 2016.* In the authors' own synthesised member check, returning to the data "proved unnecessary", though the check added data and a new concept; the paper is a method and a critique, not a demonstration that the dialogue changes the account. The two agents disagreed on the weight ("changes" against "supports"); the verifier's reading is the middle one above.
8. *Chapter 13, and the EHRC Code 2026, Chapter 7* — now read at source: it never mentions websites, and the capitalised "The duty is anticipatory" is not the Code's sentence (the lower-case phrase sits mid-sentence). Paragraph numbers differed between two passes and should be read off the page before any is printed.
9. *Chapter 12 and Stevens 1946.* Stevens ties scale type to "the basic empirical operations performed"; the "questions we intend to ask" wording is Velleman and Wilkinson's reading of him, as Chapter 12 cites it, and not his. His "pragmatic sanction" for averaging ordinal scores is quotable.
10. *Smaller:* Mogstad et al. (rank sets for the fifty largest commuting zones are "small" — ranking survives there); Becker et al. (the weight–importance gap can need negative weights to close); Yang et al. ("these 5 studies", not "all 5 studies"; Chapter 3 line 226); Berg & Pattillo (the sentences Chapter 6 quotes are in the journal abstract, not the open working paper); Weissgerber et al. 2024 (second author D.I.P. Terhorst; a single replication carrying only the null); Glaeser, Kim & Luca ("little to say about causation"; the 29.2 per cent is about establishment counts and "more accurate for denser, wealthier, and more educated ZIP codes"); Javed et al. 2010 (sixteen participants; speed, not correctness); EN 301 549 V4.1.1 clause 9 adds 9.7, "User preferences for web pages".

## How to read the evidence column

Every quotation below was returned by WebFetch, which reads the page through a small model. Two marks say how far each can be trusted.

- **STRING-VERIFIED** — the page was fetched a second time and asked YES or NO for the exact string, in a list that also held one invented control string; the string came back YES and the control came back NO. This is a model-level presence check, not a byte comparison.
- **SINGLE PASS** — returned once, not re-checked. Treat as a pointer to the passage, not as a quotation fit to print.

"Page" numbers are the reader's report of the PDF and are all SINGLE PASS.

## What the readings change, in order of weight

1. **RSS 2005 against row 7.2.** The working party permits a league table with ranking uncertainty shown and "considerable caution", and permits banding with an allowance for uncertainty. Row 7.2 forbids both and cites the report in support. The report backs Chapter 16's second ranking rule (any ordering carries its interval) and not the first.
2. **Kaliisa against row 1.7.** The proxy-moves-first finding is nine studies, five to four, and the authors call the larger effects confounded or uncontrolled. "The clearest demonstration in any field" is more than the review claims. A quotation slip sits in the same paragraph.
3. **Sjoberg against row 9.17.** 399,364 is reports from 154,957 people; the measured thing is a problem marked fixed by someone else, unverified; 11.6 per cent against 18.2 per cent; "we cannot estimate causal effects per se". The study is about being answered by action, not about being told.
4. **Birt against rows 2.23 and 9.33.** In the authors' own synthesised member check the return to the data "proved unnecessary". The paper is a method and a critique, not a demonstration that the dialogue changes the account.
5. **Mogstad against row 7.2.** The rank sets for the 50 largest commuting zones are "small". The paper marks where ranking survives as well as where it fails.
6. **Becker against row 5.5's reason.** The gap between weight and importance cannot always be closed by re-weighting; with correlated parts it needs negative weights.
7. **Yang against row 3.14.** "these 5 studies", not "all 5 studies"; and the warning reduced the effect without removing it.
8. **Berg & Pattillo and Kaminsky against row 6.11.** The open copies are the working papers. Kaminsky's record is in-sample. Berg and Pattillo's working paper reads "mixed but somewhat encouraging"; the sentences the chapter quotes belong to the journal abstract only.
9. ## What changed, in order of weight

1. **Zhi, Ottley & Metoyer 2019 (rule 11.11).** The why-cell says linking "improved recall with the slideshow layout". The paper says "We did not find any evidence for linking improving the recall task performance", and that linking made no difference to comprehension. What linking moved was self-reported engagement, time on the story and number of interactions. Analysed n is 167, on one story.
2. **Paterno et al. 2009 (rule 16.4).** "The severe rules are obeyed where set apart" rests on a tier that could not be disobeyed ("by design since this was a 'hard stop'"). The evidence that separation raises compliance is the middle tier only: 29 against 10 per cent. The lowest tier is not comparable.
3. **Tintarev & Masthoff 2012 (rule 12.26).** "Personalisation raised satisfaction" is the abstract's "may improve"; in the experiment with real trial of the items the satisfaction gain was absent and the plain baseline was the most effective, with the most opt-outs. The rule stands; the cell overstates one half.
4. **EHRC Code 2026, Chapter 7.** Now at source. The chapter never mentions websites; the capitalised "The duty is anticipatory" is not the Code's sentence.
5. **Stevens 1946.** Stevens's own sentence ties scale type to "the basic empirical operations performed"; the "questions we intend to ask" wording is not his. His "pragmatic sanction" for averaging ordinal scores is available to quote.
6. **Weissgerber et al. 2024.** Citation corrected (D.I.P. Terhorst; full title; pp. 293–318); it is a single replication and carries only the null, not "slower; less confident".
7. **Glaeser, Kim & Luca.** "We have little to say about causation"; the 29.2 per cent is about establishment counts and is "more accurate for denser, wealthier, and more educated ZIP codes".
8. **Javed et al. 2010.** Sixteen participants; speed, not correctness; time series, not profiles.
9. **EN 301 549 V4.1.1.** Clause 9 is aligned to WCAG 2.2 and adds 9.7, "User preferences for web pages" — new to the ledger.

Supported as stated, nothing to change: Zikmund-Fisher 2011 (check two sentence wordings if quoted); Lam 2008; Birt 2016; Böhme 1993; the aria-live table (1.0 edition); the Dublin Core frequency term.

## What surprised me

- **Zhi et al.'s printed means contradict the paper's own direction.** The score is "the task completion time divided by the proportion of correct responses" and "Higher scores indicates lower performance", yet the slideshow layout, reported as better, has the higher mean in both places (533 against 398 for comprehension; 139 against 97 for recall under linking). Either the labels are swapped in print or the direction is. Someone should look at Figures 5 and 6 in the PDF before the chapter leans on the layout finding at all.
- **A third of the "unreachable" list is not a library problem.** RFC 9110, DCAT 3, Media Queries 5, the aria-live table, EN 301 549 clause 9, the PsyArXiv preprint, the two HAL papers, and six PubMed Central papers (Dobres, Dong 2020, Dong 2022, van der Sijs, Spiegelhalter 2005b, probably Nanji) are all free to a person with a browser. What blocked them was the fetch tool's length cap, robots rules, and bot walls. Ten minutes at a browser closes about sixteen rows; the library is needed for about twenty.
- **PubMed Central changed behaviour mid-session.** It served Paterno 2009 in full and then returned a reCAPTCHA page for every later request.
- **The gap-rows input file is truncated** to its header. Whatever generated it should be re-run; list (b) here follows the brief's names, not the file.
- **Two ledger inconsistencies surfaced.** Javed et al. 2010 is "At source; verified" in the Chapter 3 ledger and daggered in Chapter 12; Dong et al. 2022 is "Full text; verified" in Chapter 1's ledger and "blocked" in Chapter 15's return. Both daggers may be removable without new reading.
- **Search results suggest Fingertips is being replaced** ("Read the latest updates on the new public health data service", on a Web Archive capture of 9 April 2026). If so, the quintile rule the chapter wants may now live only in the archive. Not confirmed.
- **The Albo et al. radar study compares only radial charts.** It cannot, by itself, carry a rule in favour of linear marks; Waldner et al. does that work in the row.
