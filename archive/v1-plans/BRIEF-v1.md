# Dashboard Design — the research brief
**v1 · 16 August 2026 · supersedes every prior plan · fits on two pages on purpose**

---

## Purpose

Research, chapter by chapter, **the design decisions involved in building a cutting-edge
interactive display of cultural value** — so the vibe-map / Nightlife Cultural Value Index can be
designed with the same researched confidence the Decision Book gives documents.

The reason it must exist, from the trusted partner deck: public systems measure the night through
crime, licensing, nuisance, footfall and transport, and miss belonging, trust, scene-building,
creative risk, care, talent incubation and collective joy. Producing the fuller picture currently
takes a skilled team months of manual assembly. **The instrument makes that busy-fool job
redundant: measure your area at a click.** This book researches how to design that instrument well.

"Academically robust" means the **design method** survives scrutiny — every display decision
sourced, tested where testable, tiered Fixed / Adaptable / Decorative. It does not mean doing
social science about nightlife.

---

## What is never researched

Carly's domain is an input, not a subject. **No chapter researches** licensing, night-time economy
governance, public-sector data landscapes, local government, or the sector's politics. If a chapter
needs a sector fact, it asks Carly — one line — and she answers from five years as one of three
night time economy advisors in the UK.

Her comparative sources (Thriving Places Index, Creative Footprint, EU Cultural and Creative Cities
Monitor, UK Music, the observatories, the comparative workbook's 73 studies) are read **for display
method only**: *how does this thing show its data on screen* — never *what did it find* or *is it
right about the sector*.

## The chapters — one display decision each

**Part 0 · The glance**
1. **The default view** — what does the instrument say before anyone touches it, and to whom?
2. **The two columns on one screen** — how do measured-harm data and cultural-value data share a
   display without the harm frame winning? *(The deck's slide 7–8, as a design problem.)*

**Part 1 · The display**
3. **The map** — the vibe-*map*: which kind of map (geography is one projection), what maps do
   well and badly for cultural data, and when place wants a different surface.
4. **The felt layer** — displaying vibe, atmosphere and collective joy as evidence, not decoration.
5. **Comparing places and scenes** — comparison on screen without producing a league table.
6. **The measure on screen** — how an index, a profile, a diagnostic or a ledger each *look and
   behave* when displayed, and what each display commits you to. Takes the attribution flip
   ("the visit originated with a ticket") as a measure-design question, and the dynamic census
   (measurement over time, no clipboard) as a display of time.
7. **Discovery: finding your tribe** *(added 17 Aug on steer — see
   `02-research-notes/use-case-register.md`)* — the instrument as attraction surface: a person
   finding a place that sounds like them; investors, journalists and destination organisations
   finding opportunity; scenes consenting to be found. "You cannot be what you cannot see."
8. **Gaps, uncertainty and freshness** — the honest surface: showing what is missing, how sure we
   are, and how old every number is. *(The observatory lesson and the living series live here.)*

**Part 2 · The interaction**
9. **What an interaction must earn** — the evidence on what filtering, hovering, reweighting and
   exploring actually buy and cost.
10. **Story and exploration** — authored narrative versus reader-driven discovery, and how the two
    are sequenced in one instrument.
11. **Accessible as the good version** — keyboard, screen reader, zoom and plain language as the
    design, not the alternative.

**Part 3 · The craft**
12. **The gallery** — the best interactive data work in the world, each piece analysed for what
    rule it breaks, what it substitutes and why it holds; plus a display audit of the incumbent
    instruments from the comparative sources.
13. **Make it look like art** — expressive register within evidence rules: how the instrument reads
    as nightlife's, not as "a government boring thing."

**Part 4 · The practice**
14. **Breaking these rules well** — the assembled removal tests, the override log, and the rule
    register that will eventually govern the build.

*The count moves as findings arrive — the Decision Book planned seventeen and shipped eighteen.*

## Method — per chapter, no exceptions

1. **One chapter per sprint, in order, each building on the last.** Draft → send → Carly steers →
   continue. Nothing written ahead. No documents about documents.
2. **Source sweep first**: 8–15 primary sources from design and visualisation research
   (IEEE VIS, CHI, EuroVis), practice (newsroom desks, studios, the accessibility community),
   standards where they touch display (WCAG 2.2, Government Analysis Function) — plus the
   comparative sources read for display method.
3. **Every claim carries** `evidence-status`: **measured** (a number from a study) · **evaluated**
   (assessed, not quantified) · **practice-lore** (repeated, no primary source). The field exists
   because this literature's most-quoted claims are conference anecdotes.
4. **Every finding quotes its source line or is visibly marked as inference.** Anything returned by
   a research agent is verified at source before it is written down. Both rules exist because both
   were broken.
5. **The chapter template is the book's**: the decision · the 60-second version · how the decision
   is made · rules (Rule / Why / Tier / Source) · worked example · pressure points · checklist ·
   sources. 2,500–3,500 words; over that, it splits.
6. **Conflict log per chapter** against Style Guide v1.4: agrees / conflicts / silent on. Nothing
   changes in the design system from this project.
7. **Gaps logged in four fields**; a chapter that finds no gaps was trivial or dishonest.
8. **Chat replies stay short.** The chapter file is the deliverable; the message says what changed
   and what needs steering. Nothing else.
