# Chapter 4 — The felt layer

*Dashboard Design · Part 1: The display*
*Draft for steer · 16 August 2026*

---

## The decision

**How is felt experience displayed as evidence rather than decoration?**

---

## The 60-second version

> The felt layer — vibe, belonging, care, collective joy — is the instrument's reason to exist
> and its most dangerous surface, because the two ways of failing sit either side of a narrow
> path: flatten it into a wellbeing score and it stops being true; render it as mood imagery and
> quotes-as-wallpaper and it stops being evidence.
>
> The path exists because vibe is three things, not one, and each displays differently. As
> **produced condition** it is fully researchable — atmospheres are *made*, by skilled labour,
> and that labour can be shown. As **perceived quality** it is measurable to an international
> standard: the International Organization for Standardization's (ISO's) soundscape circumplex
> includes *vibrant* as a standardised perceptual attribute of a place, rated 1–5. As **singular excess** — the thing that *"emanates from but
> exceeds the assembling of bodies"* — it is representable but not measurable, and must be
> displayed as what it is.
>
> And the house holds the rule the literature lacks. *"You can't consume a vibe. You can only
> respond, reflect, absorb, feel, sense. You are active. It needs you there, to be part of it,
> for it to exist at all."* Vibe is co-produced — which means vibe **data** is co-produced, and a
> display that shows the crowd as a subject rather than an author has reproduced the arena on a
> screen: *"you are chattel."* The scene said the same thing in the workshops, as extraction.
> **The felt layer displays with its authorship and its return visible, or it is the thing it
> set out to replace.**

---

## How the decision is made

### Step one — split the vibe, because each part displays differently

The research behind this book's raw notes established a three-way split, and it holds up against
the house's own material.

**Vibe as produced condition.** Böhme's founding move: atmospheres are *made* — by design, sound,
light, staging, welcome — and that making is observable, skilled work. Michels turned it into an
empirical programme (trace the material modulation; explore how bodies resonate; account for
stability). **[Evaluated.]** The display consequence is the one the house has been circling for
years: this layer is where the resident DJ, the lighting tech, the door person and the promoter
become visible **as skilled workers producing a measurable condition** — the "lifetime value of a
resident DJ" question, given a surface. The essay's line is the caption: *"Creating vibes is a
science."*

**Vibe as perceived quality.** ISO 12913-1 defines soundscape as the acoustic environment *"as
perceived or experienced and/or understood by people, in context"* — perception constitutive, not
incidental — and ISO/TS 12913-3 supplies an eight-attribute circumplex — *pleasant, **vibrant**,
eventful, chaotic, annoying, monotonous, uneventful, calm* — rated on 1–5 scales, with
Pleasantness and Eventfulness computed from them, reported as medians and interquartile ranges.
**[Standard; measured within its domain.]** This is the committee-room anchor: *"vibrant" is an
internationally standardised perceptual attribute of a place.* One honest caveat travels with it:
the standard's home is urban soundscape assessment; applying it inside venues and to night-time
scenes is an extension, and the chapter marks it as one.

**Vibe as singular excess.** Anderson's affective atmospheres — *"singular affective qualities
that emanate from but exceed the assembling of bodies"* — with three deliberate ambiguities and
no method offered. Garcia-Mispireta's dancefloor ethnography shows what a careful account looks
like and warns that the felt sense of togetherness is *"vague, fluid, and may hide exclusions and
injustices."* **[Evaluated.]** This part is displayed as **testimony, narrative and expressive
encoding** — never as a number — and it carries the Decision Book's Chapter 7 licence: the
expressive version is permitted *because* the precise version ships alongside.

The Fixed rule: **the three parts are displayed as three registers and never collapsed.** A
"vibe score" that blends a labour observation, a circumplex median and a feeling is a category
error wearing a KPI's clothes — the flattening your own metric guardrail already names: culture
reduced to a delivery mechanism.

### Step two — use the displays the scene has already invented

The Vibe Setting Framework's workshops (**unpublished — confidential until release**) were not
just consultation. They were **display prototypes**, and better ones than most of the literature.

Participants stood on a line across a room — agree, undecided, disagree — against three
statements (*"I feel welcome and able to be myself in Bristol music spaces"* among them). That is
a **human dot plot**: a distribution displayed in bodies, readable at a glance, self-placed. It
translates directly to screen: the felt layer's natural chart form is **the distribution of
placed selves** — marks people put down themselves — not a bar of averages. Self-placement on the
co-created map is the same form spatially. One hundred and twenty-five post-its distilled to ten
principles is qualitative aggregation with its working shown.

Two display rules fall straight out:

- **Show the distribution, never just the centre.** The workshop finding was "largely towards
  agree, with consensus that more work is needed" — a shape, not a score. Belonging data
  displayed as a mean deletes the people at the disagree end, who are the finding.
- **A self-placed mark is a different object from an assigned one, and the display says which
  it is.** This is the verified-versus-inferred discipline from the grants work, applied to
  feelings.

**And the form scales up: the digital human dot plot is a collection instrument, not just a
chart.** *"Insert yourself into the map"* becomes measurable the moment the display invites the
mark — "what does your city sound like?" asked as a playable survey, each answer a placed self
that feeds the city's felt distribution in return. **[House steer, 16 August 2026.]** Three
things make this more than a feature idea:

- **It is the studio model in miniature.** A city-based study plus audience participation means
  the fans themselves build the evidence of how nightlife culture feels in their place — advocacy
  hand in hand with the community and the people who make things happen, and repeatable per
  place. The policy channel already exists: the Commons culture committee's Fan-Led Review of
  Live and Electronic Music (April 2026) gave fan voice its own chapter and named Bristol Nights
  in it — fan-contributed evidence is now a recognised input, not an anecdote. **[Published.]**
- **Belonging becomes a definitive metric — and it reorders the hierarchy.** Self-placed marks
  measure attachment, not volume, so they do not scale with population. A town with a strong
  scene and no "music city" reputation — Hull is the house's example — can genuinely outscore
  London on belonging, because the metric is intensity per participant, not activity per square
  mile. That is Chapter 3's normalisation rule arriving at city scale: the aggregate flatters
  the biggest player; the placed-self distribution lets the strongest *scene* be seen.
- **Two honesty conditions travel with it, or it breaks.** First, the yield is a
  **distribution in register two** — perceived quality, self-selected, provenance stated on the
  face — and any headline drawn from it says so; it never blends with the other registers into a
  vibe-o-meter. Second, a participatory surface invites manipulation: Queering the Map was
  brigaded offline before it had moderation. **Moderation and consent are designed in before
  launch, not after the first incident.**

### Step three — display belonging as cues, because that is where it lives

The workshops located belonging precisely: *"more than presence… feeling recognised, safe
enough, useful, welcome and able to contribute without having to become someone else"* — and,
critically, in **small cues**: *"who is visible, who is promoted, who is expected to know the
language, who can access the space, whose genre is taken seriously, whose behaviour is judged,
and whose contribution is quietly missed."*

The essay holds the same truth at street level: the bar staff remembering an order, *"let me
charge that for you"*, *"you ok to get home?"* — *"I came alone, I left with friends."*

The display consequence: **felt evidence is concrete or it is nothing.** A belonging indicator
displays as the cue itself — named, counted where countable, quoted where not — never as an
abstraction ("community cohesion: 7.2"). The cue list above is, in effect, a community-authored
indicator set; the instrument's job is to give each cue a surface, not to launder them into a
composite. This is also what makes the felt layer *auditable*: a hostile reader can argue with
"whose genre is taken seriously" as a claim about programming data; they can only dismiss a
cohesion score.

### Step four — the co-production rule, which is the chapter's spine

The essay's theory of vibe is participatory all the way down: *"Music is born from place because
it's a conversation. It needs response, feedback."* The MC redirects the set because of one
dancer. The crowd is not the audience of the vibe; it is the medium. *"It needs you there, to be
part of it, for it to exist at all."* **[House axiom.]**

Now the display consequence, and it is the rule this chapter exists to state. **A felt layer
displayed *about* people converts participants into product** — the arena on a screen, the
Costa-Coffee-ification of the very thing being defended. And the scene named the institutional
version in the workshops, unprompted: people described *"relationships with institutions as
feeling extractive: where knowledge, culture, credibility or community insight is taken from the
scene, but the value does not always flow back to the people and places who created it."*

So the felt layer carries three visible properties or it does not publish:

1. **Authorship** — who said it, who placed it, in their register (design principle 9: *"speak
   the language of the scene"* — testimony is not translated into policy voice).
2. **Consent and control** — the CARE-shaped question the workshop asked itself: *"what consent
   processes are needed, especially for informal or underground networks that may not want
   visibility?"* Some of the felt layer is **community-held, not published** — and per Chapter 2,
   that non-publication is rendered as a labelled gap, not silence.
3. **Return** — design principle 3, *visible, evidenced listening*: *"People need to see that
   their voice changed something… what was heard, what changed, what could not change, and
   why."* The felt layer's provenance chip has a fourth field the quantitative layers don't
   need: **what this changed**. That is how a display proves it is a conversation and not a
   harvest — *"the CBS needs to show its workings"*, as a design requirement.

### Step five — when the felt and the counted disagree, that is a finding

The mixed-methods literature names three outcomes of integrating strands: confirmation,
expansion, and **discordance** — and treats discordance as a result to report, not a failure to
resolve. **[Evaluated — Fetters, Curry & Creswell.]** Pre-commit to it.

For this instrument the discordant case is the important one, and the 78/32 finding shows its
shape: the room that is economically marginal and culturally load-bearing. A small venue failing
on every counted metric while the felt layer shows it holding a scene together is not a data
quality problem. It is the instrument's central story, and the display must let the two layers
disagree **on the same screen** — Chapter 2's juxtaposition rule, now with the felt layer as one
of the columns.

## Worked example

*Illustrative — one night's felt layer on one screen; fixes nothing about the design.*

**The subject:** a monthly night at a small room — the kind that is invisible in aggregate
economics and load-bearing in the scene.

**Register one — produced.** The labour panel: who made the atmosphere tonight. Resident DJ
(seven years), door person, lighting, the promoter's unpaid hours. Counted where countable,
named where consented. Caption: *creating vibes is a science.*

**Register two — perceived.** The circumplex panel: attendees' 1–5 ratings of the night on the
eight attributes, shown as a **distribution of placed marks** — each mark one person's own
placement — with the median drawn and the spread visible. Provenance chip: self-reported ·
n stated · instrument named · *extension of ISO 12913-3 to an indoor venue, stated*.

**Register three — excess.** One piece of testimony, in the author's own words and register,
displayed at full evidential weight with attribution as consented — plus the precise version
alongside: the counted cues (free-entry tier existed · first-time performers on the bill · night
running n years).

**The fourth field, on everything:** *what this changed* — "this night's data was cited in the
venue's meanwhile-use extension case, March 2026." The display proving it is a conversation.

**The five-second test:** a licensing-frame reader should come away saying *"that room is doing
skilled, valued work"* — not *"nice quotes."* If the felt layer reads as decoration, it has
failed even if every number on the screen is right.

## Pressure points

**Where there is real latitude.**

*The expressive register.* The sound-bounce idea — vibration, reflection, response — is a native
visual language for this layer that no incumbent owns: encodings that answer the data as a room
answers a set. Chapter 12's territory, fully licensed here provided the precise version ships.

*In-room display.* The workshops themselves showed it: the felt layer displays best where it was
made — spectrum lines, placed marks, walls of post-its. The screen version can inherit those
forms rather than replacing them, and a live-prototype phase alongside the Community Benefit
Society build is the natural place to run both together.

*Sonification.* A layer about sound may legitimately be *heard*. Unevaluated territory —
flagged, not licensed.

**Where it looks sophisticated and fails.**

*The vibe-o-meter.* One blended score per place. Every failure in this chapter at once, plus
Chapter 2's forbidden composite.

*Sentiment-scraping the scene.* Harvesting social posts to score "mood" fails authorship,
consent and return in one move — extraction with extra steps.

*Joy as stock photography.* Mood imagery standing where evidence should be: meaning in the
decorative layer.

*Quotes as wallpaper.* Testimony set small, ghosted, or as section furniture. If it carries
meaning it is evidence and displays at evidential weight; if it does not, it should not be there.

*The anonymised-but-identifiable mark.* In a scene of forty people, one placed mark with a genre
and a night attached is a name. Chapter 8's disclosure rules bind hardest exactly here.

**What proves a break works.** Show the screen to someone whose testimony is on it. If they feel
*represented* — their words, their register, their mark, and what it changed — the layer is
evidence. If they feel *used*, it is extraction, whatever the design awards say. That is the
dancefloor test with the sharpest possible edge, and it is the one test in this book that only
the scene can run.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every felt item classified: produced, perceived, or excess — and displayed in that register
- [ ] No blended felt score anywhere
- [ ] Atmosphere labour visible as labour
- [ ] Perceived quality shown as distributions of placed marks, medians and spread, n stated
- [ ] The ISO extension stated as an extension
- [ ] Every expressive display ships its precise version
- [ ] Self-placed and assigned marks visibly distinct
- [ ] Belonging shown as named cues, not abstraction scores
- [ ] Authorship, consent status and return visible on all felt data
- [ ] "What this changed" populated, or honestly empty and shown as such
- [ ] Community-held material rendered as a labelled gap
- [ ] Discordance with the counted layer displayed, not reconciled away
- [ ] Disclosure checked at scene scale, not city scale
- [ ] No testimony in the decorative layer
- [ ] The represented-not-used test run with a contributor

## Style Guide v1.4 — conflict log

**Agrees.** "Evidence with soul" is this chapter's thesis in three words. §03's register
("culturally literate… never performing authenticity") governs how testimony is set. §06's
never-make-weak-evidence-look-stronger holds the line between registers two and three.

**Conflicts.** None found.

**Silent on.** Displaying qualitative data at evidential weight (the guide covers quotes
editorially, not evidentially); consent and return as display elements; distributions of
self-placed marks as a chart form. All three are candidates for the guide's eventual dashboard
section, via the design-system conversation.
