### B. What the chapter teaches

The chapter answers one question (line 20): before a place is scored, how do you ask a body of data how value is made and for whom, and how far can each method be trusted. It teaches a newcomer eight things in sequence: that behaviour is evidence but every behavioural record names who is in it; that diversity, access and welcome are measurable if the weighting is declared; that "survived" and "resilient" are narrower words than they sound; that the hollow success has names in forestry and medicine; that every activity figure is gross until the appraisal arithmetic is shown and a price may be a rent; that a dashboard sits on the first rung of Pearl's ladder and must say so; that a pattern found through a filter bar is a multiple-comparisons result and a change is a signal only against its own seasonal history; and that small places need intervals, shrinkage and two distinct reasons for an asterisk. The takeaway is a ten-line claim record (lines 422–431) under every statement of value, where "not done" is written rather than hidden.
