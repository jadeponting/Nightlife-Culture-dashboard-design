## B. What the chapter teaches

The chapter takes one line of the brief's failure register ("it fails if they do not recognise their place") and shows it is not one failure but four senses (geography, self, account, standing) and five testable failures (wrong unit, not in it, the account is wrong, the counted won, you lost). For a newcomer building a page about a place, it decides: the statistical unit is a declared choice stated with what it hides, because no UK unit was built around what residents recognise and changing the unit can flip a finding; a reader looks for a named place first; the counted and missed columns sit side by side on separate scales with equal finish, the missed column first as an endorsement rather than because "repeating the opposing frame strengthens it" (a reason it withdraws against the corrections literature); what a place *is* is never ranked, as a declared stance, while what it *has* is compared through the comparability gate; discovery is matching on the reader's own weights, never an ordering by size; and anything cropped from a page carries its provenance inside the frame. It leaves a ten-line recognition record under every place-page. A newcomer would take away that recognition is mostly a question of unit, order, labels and frames, and that the house's best-known rule survives on different grounds than it was first given.
