## B. What the chapter teaches (as a reader)

The chapter decides what a dashboard owes a reader before they touch anything: that the first screen is a sentence to a named reader, that nothing is asked before something is given, that the phone is the first version, that every interaction must name what it buys and what it costs, that a state a reader reaches must be citable as a dated snapshot and not merely a link, and that a map is the hardest first screen because dark reads as more and small areas are missed by a third. It is for the person building or commissioning the Vibe Map's surface — a creative technologist, a designer, a research partner — and it hands them a twelve-line "face sheet" per view as the tool. A newcomer takes away one memorable claim per section: titles are read first and remembered over charts; interaction does not make people more accurate; links rot and content drifts; a source line is a signal not to check; design the small screen first or the phone gets what is left.
