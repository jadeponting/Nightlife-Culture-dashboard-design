## B. What the chapter teaches

The chapter decides that "vibe" is three constructs, not one (made, perceived, excess), that only the perceived register has a tested instrument, and that any of the four families of method (participatory, relational, traces, signals) is evidence of something narrower than the place: of who marked, who was in the room, whom the platform has, how people appear to others. It is written for a first-time reader who will build or commission a felt layer, and it tells them that a felt measure is a distribution with its sample on its face, that recognition by the people described is two of five kinds of validity evidence and not the whole, and that a disagreement between felt and counted is reported rather than resolved. A newcomer takes away a vocabulary (construct, descriptor, indicator, index, register), a twelve-line measure record, and the ONS personal wellbeing measures as the yardstick for what building a felt measure properly costs.
