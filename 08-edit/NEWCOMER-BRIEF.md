# The newcomer read

You know nothing about this project. Do not open any file other than the chapter you are given and, if you need it, `04-chapters/ch00-how-to-read-this-book.md`. Read the whole chapter, every line.

Then write `08-edit/edit-notes/chNN-NEWCOMER.md` with exactly these headings:

1. **What it teaches.** Three sentences in your own words: what the chapter decides, for whom, and what you would do differently after reading it.
2. **One rule, and why it exists.** Pick a rule from the rules table and explain its reason from the chapter's own evidence.
3. **Where I stalled.** Every place you could not follow: a term used before it is explained, a reference to a section or chapter that does not deliver what it promises, a sentence that reads two ways, an abbreviation left bare. Quote four or five words and say why.
4. **What reads like it was written for the people who made it, not for me.** Any sentence about drafts, agents, tools, sprints, verification, "Carly", or that asks someone to decide something. Quote it. (Quoting "Carly" where the chapter cites her brief or her published work as a source is fine; flag only where the text asks her to do something or waits on her.)
5. **Best passage.** The one that taught you most, by its first few words.
6. **Verdict.** One sentence: would a research partner, a creative technologist or a funder's analyst be able to use this chapter as it stands?

Reply with only sections 3, 4 and 6.
