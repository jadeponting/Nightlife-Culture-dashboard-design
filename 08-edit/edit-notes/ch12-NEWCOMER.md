# Chapter 12 — Newcomer read

## 1. What it teaches

The chapter decides that "people must recognise their place" in a public dashboard about places is not one requirement but five: the geographic unit must be declared and more than one offered; the reader's place must be findable by a name people actually use; what systems count about a place and what a scene holds but nobody counts must sit side by side with equal craft and no shared axis; what a place *is* is never ranked while what it *has* is compared only through a stated comparability check; and anything screenshotted out of the page must still carry its own context. It is written for whoever builds and governs a place-based dashboard (here, a nightlife one) and for the researchers who have to defend its evidence. After reading it I would stop treating a ward or an LSOA as "the neighbourhood", would never publish a filled radar chart or a sorted all-places list, and would put title, unit, source, date and the comparison's wording inside every chart image rather than beside it.

## 2. One rule, and why it exists

"No ordering the house makes in discovery is by size or popularity." The chapter's evidence is Salganik, Dodds and Watts's 14,341-person artificial music market: when the display moved from a grid with download counts to a single column sorted by popularity, both inequality and unpredictability of success rose, so the sort order itself changed what succeeded. Singh and Joachims add the mechanism, that exposure is "largely determined by position in the ranking", with a relevance gap of 0.03 turning into 30 per cent less exposure. A sorted list is therefore not a neutral view of the places but a treatment applied to them, and it reproduces "an aggregation of the biggest", which is the failure the brief rejects.

## 3. Where I stalled

- "The brief's fourth failure" (60-second version) and "The brief's eighth failure" (section 7). Which brief? It is never named, and I cannot tell how many failures it lists or what the others are.
- "Version 1 said the counted column" and some forty further uses of "version 1". The only definition is in Sources: "Version 1, Chapters 2, 5 and 7 (archived)". A newcomer reads a whole chapter arguing with a document they have never been told exists.
- "the house", "the house's declaration", "house stance". Used as a proper noun for an organisation throughout. Nightlife Culture is cited as a source but the text never says the house *is* Nightlife Culture.
- "with the gate on the face" (the opening question). "The gate" is not explained until section 6, and "on the face" is an idiom for "printed on the chart" that is never glossed. The whole opening question is one 130-word sentence with six "so that" clauses; I had to read it three times.
- "the deck's two columns", "The deck's slides 7 and 8", "The trusted-partner deck". What deck, shown to whom? Sources call it "House; axiom", which explains nothing.
- "the Decision Book's ARG-12", "LAY-08", "DAT-18", "DAT-21", "TBL-17". Codes from a document a newcomer does not have; ARG-12 and LAY-08 get quoted, the others do not.
- "within the poster rule". Used five times (sections 5, 6, 11, 13, rules table). Never explained; presumably Chapter 11's. I cannot judge whether a bridging sentence passes it.
- "the three-phones time (Chapter 11)". Bare. I guessed it is a time limit measured in some way with three phones, but the test in section 10 depends on it.
- "Chapter 10's walk", "the query-time walk", "run with the walk". Rules depend on it; nothing here says what a walk is.
- "the forum", "Chapter 10's forum", "the forum's October session". A governance body is assumed and never described in this chapter.
- "the safeguards record", "the data contract", "the harm frame" and "a holder of the harm frame". Four house artefacts named as if familiar; the tests in section 10 cannot be run without knowing who a "holder of the harm frame" is.
- "the three user groups". Section 10 and "What proves a break works" run every test with them; only section 13 hints (scene member, licensing officer, funder) and never says those are the three.
- "Form 696 enters here as Carly's frame". Form 696 is a bare reference (the former Metropolitan Police event risk-assessment form) and "Carly" has not been introduced as a person.
- "†" appears from the 60-second version onwards and is only explained in the Sources preamble 470 lines later.
- "Fixed (requirement)", "Fixed (sequence)", "Adaptable (house default)", "Decorative". The Tier column's vocabulary is never defined in the chapter.
- "ELS QMI", "the ELS cluster method", "IoD2025", "SIMD", "OCSI", "MHCLG", "CBPR principle 7", "GCPH", "ESS Code P14", "BUAs", "OAC". ELS is never spelled out anywhere, including Sources ("Explore Local Statistics QMI" appears, but the link to "ELS" is never made). SIMD, IoD2025, CBPR and GCPH appear bare in the rules table.
- "the Analysis Function". Cited as a standard a dozen times; never expanded to the UK Government Analysis Function.
- "the eight criteria", "which of the eight criteria required harmonisation". The gate's eight criteria are the heart of rules 19 and 20 and are never listed; Sources sends me to "Comparative Methodology v1.0" via "Style Guide §12".
- "Section 1 splits the word into four senses and section 10 into five tests; the headline findings fall under five heads". Four senses, five heads, five failures: the mapping is never drawn. Sense three (the account) is not one of the five heads in the 60-second version at all; it surfaces only in section 9.
- "The counted column first, the value column as the reply" (section 2) and "Value-first survives". "Value column" is used as a synonym for "missed column" without saying so; elsewhere the same column is "assets" and "abundance". Four names for one thing.
- "a reader from a place whose counted column is thin". Reads two ways. In the nightlife case the counted column is incidents and complaints, so "thin" could mean a well-behaved place or a place the systems ignore. The chapter means the second; nothing tells me that.
- "Version 1's Chapter 7 built a discovery layer" (section 7) beside "Chapter 7's reactivity (Espeland & Sauder)" and "Chapter 7's open choice". Two different Chapter 7s in one section, one archived and one current, with no signal which is which.
- "Chapter 9's discordant cell", "Chapter 4's six kinds of nothing", "Chapter 6's family of counted activity measures", "Chapter 7's position-in-spread". Each is a concept imported by label only.
- "the missed column first as a declared endorsement and never alone" (truth 3). "Never alone" could attach to the endorsement or to the column.
- "named in section 1's third sense" (Sources, on Sieber, Elwood and Peluso). Section 1's third sense does not name them. Broken cross-reference.
- "Chapter 13 tests the departure against the criteria". Which criteria? The rule on in-frame text is left hanging on an unseen chapter.
- "Beneath each place's page the chapter leaves a ninth record". Ninth of what is not explained until section 11, 280 lines later.

## 4. What reads like it was written for the people who made it, not for me

- "where the house chooses the scene scale as the landing unit (a choice Chapter 11 left to the studio)" (section 3).
- "The prototype can run the test the literature has not." (section 4) and "that is the prototype's question." (section 6). A prototype I have not been told about is assigned homework.
- "Two mock pages, one with the columns set equally and one with the missed column rougher, read by the three user groups, would be the first test." (section 5). A task for the team.
- "So the rule ... is withdrawn as an evidence claim, and ARG-12 is logged as a conflict for the Decision Book." (section 5). Internal document maintenance.
- "The rule could not be found in the Othering & Belonging Institute's public narrative essays; if the house document attributes it there, the attribution is unconfirmed." (section 5). A note to whoever maintains the house document.
- "typography to be checked by eye" (section 5, Rendering absence). A production note left in the text.
- "The case version 1 took from Chapter 1 is also corrected." (section 5). Correcting a sibling chapter in front of the reader.
- "the paper's own breakdown of the remainder does not reconcile with its total, and is not printed"; "the '−0.99 to +0.99' that circulates could not be verified and is not printed"; "the 2021 supergroup names could not be verified at source and are not printed" (sections 3 and 6). Verification log entries. Useful to the editor; to me they describe absences I would not have noticed.
- "the numbers are the studio's to set" (section 10); "the thresholds (25/50/75 are one precedent) and the number of people behind them are the studio's" (section 12); "(a choice the studio would make, shown here only so the line has content)" (section 11). Each hands a decision to someone in the room.
- The whole of section 12, "Where the choice is yours", is addressed to the house as the second person: "Whether to let readers draw their own unit", "whether the house classifies places at all is a choice this chapter does not make". Fine as a design section, but it presumes the reader is the decision-maker.
- "Chapters 4 to 11 each left a record; whether they are one document is settled in Chapter 16." (section 11) and, in the rules table, "Nine records now; whether one document is for the end". Book-assembly planning.
- "Who counts as the scene's members ... is the forum's question and not this chapter's." (section 9). Passes a decision to a body the reader has not met.
- "Whether being shown one's page in the room reads as respect or as surveillance is Chapter 9's open question and remains open." (section 9).
- "Form 696 enters here as Carly's frame and as version 1's test, kept" (section 7) and the rules table's "Nightlife Culture (Carly's frame)". This cites her rather than tasks her, so it is permitted under the brief, but "Carly" is an unintroduced first name in a chapter that otherwise names every source in full.
- "A break in any Fixed rule above is logged with these two results attached." (What proves a break works). Process instruction to the team.
- Sources preamble: "Stevens's 1946 paper and Birt's member-checking procedure were read in the library pass of 7 October 2026"; "All fetched 6 October 2026"; "the pledge page itself was not re-reached"; "Version 1, Chapters 2, 5 and 7 (archived). Positions tested, not sources." A working diary.
- Repeated "could not be reached", "not reachable", "unreached" (Lynch, Coulton 2001, Kennedy & Hill, Lincoln & Guba, Bennett & Peterson, Fattore et al. 2012, Motulsky, Sieber, Elwood, Peluso). Honest, but reads as a to-do list for the next research pass.

## 5. Best passage

"The reading is not that small units are true and large ones false." The three sentences that follow it (section 3, end of the MAUP subsection) are the clearest statement in the chapter of why a unit is a declared choice and what the page owes the reader because of it.

## 6. Verdict

A research partner could use it with the rules table, section 11 and the Sources as their map, but a creative technologist would be slowed at nearly every paragraph by undefined house vocabulary (the poster rule, the walk, the forum, the gate's eight criteria, the tier labels, the Decision Book codes) and by the running argument with an archived "version 1", and a funder's analyst would not get past the opening question; as it stands the chapter is usable only by someone who has read Chapters 7, 9, 10 and 11 and the house documents first.
