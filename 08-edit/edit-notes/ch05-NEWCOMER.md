# Chapter 5 — newcomer read

## 1. What it teaches

The chapter decides that a single score built from several counted things is a model, not a finding, and that six judgements inside it (normalisation, weights, aggregation, robustness, validity, the audit of what the measure does once published) must travel with the number in a standard nine-line card one tap from the score. It is written for anyone who would build, commission or read a composed score of a place's culture or night, and it refuses to settle whether such a score should be built at all. After reading it I would not publish a rank without its interval, I would compute and state what one part is worth in another's units before showing any total, and I would treat "the scene recognises itself in it" as a check on facts and resonance rather than proof the thing measured exists.

## 2. One rule, and why it exists

"Show the effective importance of each part beside its nominal weight." The chapter's evidence is Paruolo, Saisana and Saltelli's audit of the Times Higher Education ranking: an indicator given 40% of the weight explained 81% of the variance in the result, and one given 20% explained 21%. The mechanism is correlation: when the parts move together the weights cannot separate them and become "little more than cosmetic". The Global Innovation Index developers now say in their own report that their weights are "scaling coefficients and not importance coefficients". So a dashboard that lists weights has told the reader the recipe and not the dish; showing both closes the gap.

## 3. Where I stalled

- "Kind and tier as in Chapter 0" (rules table preamble). *Standard*, *Evidence*, *Practice*, *Reading*, *Nightlife Culture*, **Fixed**, Adaptable and *Decorative* are used from section 11 onwards as if I know them. Section 13 at least pairs each kind with an example, but "Fixed (statement of the record)" and "Fixed (sequence)" versus "Fixed (requirement)" are never distinguished, and I could not say what the difference is.
- "is, on this chapter's evidence, Practice" (section 11) and "marked Practice" (section 12). A capitalised noun used as a verdict in running prose. I guessed it means "a convention without evidence behind it" but only from context.
- "(abstract †)" (first at section 7, "The symmetry"; also "read at abstract" in the 60-second version). The dagger is explained only in the Sources preamble at the very end. I met it eight or nine times before learning what it meant.
- "the studio" (60-second version, "the studio's own sector"; section 9 "The studio's position... carried into the brief"). Never introduced. Nor is "the brief". "Nightlife Culture" first appears as a rule kind in section 12, not as an organisation.
- "Carly's failure register says the same" (section 9). Who Carly is and what the failure register is are not given in the chapter. Reads as a cross-reference to an internal document.
- "The Decision Book has no rules on composites" and "the rules table above... is the candidate chapter" (What the field does not know). What the Decision Book is, and what "candidate chapter" means (of the Decision Book? of this book?), is not said. The Sources entry adds "(design-decisions skill)", which is a tool name.
- "Log the break in the deviation entry Chapter 16 describes" (What proves a break works). "Deviation entry" is bare.
- "the two-significant-digits rule Chapter 3 took from Ehrenberg" (section 6). Ehrenberg is not introduced here; the rule is only recoverable from the ONS quotation that follows.
- "Chapter 2's official reader, answered in the official reader's currency" (section 10). "The official reader" is a term from another chapter, used as if familiar.
- "minus one at the top, zero (the geometric mean) in the middle, one (plain addition) at the bottom" (section 5, Legatum). Top and bottom of what? I first read it as a scale of exponents, which made no sense (the exponent "rises" to one at the bottom). The table later makes clear it is the index hierarchy: domain level at the top, indicators at the bottom. The sentence reads two ways.
- "a prosperity index uses means that 'punish weakness'" (60-second version). "Means" read first as methods, then as averages. Minor, but it is in the summary.
- "three of the five place indices with published methods" (60-second version and section 10). The table has six rows. I worked out that the ONS dashboard is excluded because it is not an index, but the text should say "five of the six instruments in the table".
- "the refusal test version 1 posed" (section 12, line 5). The test is named before it is stated, and the name is never explained. Why "refusal"? Because the display refuses to answer, or the studio refuses the number? Section 15 calls it "the studio's values test", which helped.
- "intensity not volume; identity never merged with conditions" (Sources, version 1 entry). Two slogans that appear nowhere else in the chapter.
- "For ARWU the main effects Si are more similar to each other than the nominal weights" (section 4, quoted). ARWU is the Shanghai ranking's acronym and Si is the main-effect symbol; neither is glossed, and the quotation sits in the sentence that introduces the Shanghai result.
- "the MCTAI median value" (section 6, quoted). Unglossed inside the quotation; I inferred Monte Carlo TAI.
- "stable and reliable patterns in SWB" (section 8, quoted). SWB never spelled out.
- Bare abbreviations in the rules table and the record: "IoD 2019", "SSF 2009", "HDR 2010", "PASC 2003", "ACE", "SPI methodology", "Code of Practice 3.0", "ONS QMI". Each institution is spelled out somewhere in the body, but the abbreviations themselves are never introduced, and IoD, SSF, HDR, PASC and ACE appear only in abbreviated form in the tables.
- "the handbook's toolbox" (section 5). Only the Sources entry explains that this is Part II of the handbook.
- "which Chapter 3 found most readers will not do unaided" (section 11). I cannot check it from here; it is load-bearing for the composite-versus-dashboard choice.
- "its design is Chapter 11's and the design system's" (section 12). "The design system" is bare.
- The Campbell and Fiske parenthetical (section 8, "Convergent and discriminant"): "(the original was not reachable; the definitions are the Standards' and a methods text's, and the one phrase of theirs ... is as quoted by Rönkkö and Cho)". A provenance note that interrupts the one sentence where the two terms are defined; I had to read it three times.

## 4. What reads like it was written for the people who made it, not for me

- "the illustration that version 1 of this book (the studio's earlier draft of this book, now archived) carried and this chapter has now read at the Joint Research Centre's own reproduction" (section 5). Draft history and verification in the body.
- "and all of what follows was read at the authors' abstracts" (section 7, "The symmetry").
- "(secondary)" after Borsboom (section 8) and Lincoln and Guba (section 9); "David Thomas's 2017 review (secondary)". Source-status flags in the prose.
- "the original was not reachable; the definitions are the Standards' and a methods text's" (section 8).
- "The report cited is the delivery team's; the independent academic critiques were not reachable." (section 10, ACE).
- "(the OECD's own methodology page was unreadable; confirmed at secondary level and from OECD meta-text)" (section 10, table, Better Life Index row).
- "the 2024 methodology PDF had no readable text" (Sources, Thriving Places).
- "This section reads instruments for their published method only ... and never for their findings about any place" (section 10 opening), "reports nothing about any city" (Creative Footprint), "The chapter says nothing about whether its judgements of any place are right" (same), "No finding about any city is reported" (Sources). Four statements of the same internal policy; one would do, and it reads as a rule the authors set themselves.
- "Carly's failure register says the same" (section 9). Cites an internal document rather than published work.
- "the chapter should say so before building anything" (section 1) and "the book must say so with the same force" (section 7). The chapter instructing itself.
- "the choice is the studio's" (section 11); "the studio's framework keeps the choice open and this chapter does not close it" (section 14).
- "Version 1 of this book stated one Fixed rule for whatever form the studio chooses" (section 12) and "the refusal test version 1 posed and this chapter keeps as a Nightlife Culture rule" (section 12, line 5).
- "poses a question the studio has not yet had to answer" (section 15).
- All of section 14, "Where the choice is yours", is addressed to the studio as decider; in particular "**Whether the sector composite's method audit sits here or in Chapter 15.**" is an editorial question about where to put a passage in the book, left in the published text.
- "Log the break in the deviation entry Chapter 16 describes." (What proves a break works). Process instruction.
- "The Decision Book has no rules on composites ... the rules table above ... is the candidate chapter." "No house position on whether the measure is one-tailed" (What the field does not know). These are about the state of the studio's own documents, not about what the field does not know.
- Sources: "design-decisions skill", "nightlife-culture-design skill"; "verified (the 80% figure restored to its full form)"; "verified (the consequential-aspect quotation restored in full; a sentence not in the report removed)"; "At source but **no quotation used**: sentences first attributed to it could not be found in the document"; "(reconstructed — wording flagged)". These are the edit log of the chapter, not a bibliography.
- "The mashup paper prints '$0.50' in one sentence and '$0.51' in the next; this chapter uses the companion paper's '$0.51'." (Sources). Useful to a checker, odd for a reader.
- "Fixed (statement of the record)" repeated seven times in section 13. Internal tier vocabulary doing no work for an outside reader.

## 5. Best passage

"The price list can be computed from any additive recipe, and it is worth seeing how..." (section 5). The years-in-operation against events-per-month example turned "normalisation sets the exchange rate" from an assertion into something I could redo on paper, and it is the passage that made the refusal test make sense.

## 6. Verdict

A research partner could use sections 1 to 13 and the rules table as they stand, and a creative technologist could build from the nine-line card and the checklist; a funder's analyst would get the argument but would be slowed by the undefined tier vocabulary, the daggers, the bare abbreviations in the tables, and the studio-facing material in sections 14, "What the field does not know" and the Sources notes, which belong in an internal log rather than the chapter.
