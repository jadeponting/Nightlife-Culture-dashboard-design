# Chapter 7 — Newcomer read

## 1. What it teaches

The chapter decides that no comparison on a dashboard — place against place, this period against last, before against after, or present against projected — may be shown without a ten-line "comparison record" saying what was held equal, who chose the comparators, how uncertain each figure is, what design sits behind any claim of cause, and on what terms any projection is made; and it bans league tables and position bands of small places outright. It is written for whoever designs or signs off a dashboard of districts and venues — a studio and its designers are named — and, less directly, for the reader who has to trust it. After reading it I would stop sorting bar charts by value and calling the order a finding, would never let a vertical "policy introduced" marker stand without the eight rival explanations beside it, and would write "after", "departed from its trend" or "contributed to" where I used to write "because".

## 2. One rule, and why it exists

**"No league table of places on any measure, and no bands of position."** The chapter's evidence runs in three steps. Rankings change what they rank: Espeland and Sauder found that law-school rankings "do not simply reflect an underlying social hierarchy but in fact play a crucial role in creating this hierarchy", because position is zero-sum and institutions reorganise to chase it. Ranks are noise at the margins: Goldstein and Spiegelhalter's 325 schools gave "about two thirds of all possible comparisons do not allow separation", and Marshall and Spiegelhalter's fertility clinics changed rank between years "even though their live birth rate did not change significantly". And the institutions that have audited their own rankings have withdrawn them: the World Bank's external panel said "Remove the aggregate index and country rankings", and the NHS tells users "It is inappropriate to rank trusts according to their SHMI." The chapter is candid that the extension of this to every measure on a dashboard of small places is its own reading rather than a published standard, and that the Royal Statistical Society source supports the interval, not the prohibition; the funnel plot (section 5) is offered as the display that keeps the information and drops the hierarchy.

## 3. Where I stalled

- **"the Vibe Map should make"** (§1). The chapter's one named object is never explained — a product? a dashboard? a project? — and it does not recur, so I cannot infer it.
- **"the studio and its designers"** (§1, §12, §13, rules table). "The studio" is the chapter's addressee and decision-maker, used a dozen times and never identified.
- **"†"** in the 60-second version (two daggers in the first paragraph). The symbol is used before §3 explains it as "a source read at the authors' abstract or summary only".
- **"(read once)"** from §4 onward. Explained only in the Sources preamble at the very end of the chapter, and nowhere signposted.
- **"their SHMI"** in the 60-second version. Bare abbreviation; expanded as the Summary Hospital-level Mortality Indicator only in §3.
- **"the 1976 ESP"** (§4). Never expanded (European Standard Population, I assume).
- **"The modern method, STL"** (§6). Never expanded or described beyond its parameters.
- **"ARIMA modelling"** and **"Holt–Winters"** (§6, "How long before a trend may be read"). Both bare; a non-statistician reader has no footing.
- **"PSM estimation results"** (§8, matching). The text never says propensity score matching; only the Sources list does.
- **"ITSA does not require"** (§7, Magenta Book quote). Never glossed as interrupted time series analysis.
- **"the Magenta Book"** (§7 onward) and **"The Green Book's February 2026 edition"** (§9). Neither is ever said to be HM Treasury's evaluation and appraisal guidance in the body; only the Sources list says so. A non-UK reader stalls hard.
- **"FPP3 §3"**, **"DIME"**, **"J-PAL"**, **"JRC C3 2017/2019"** (rules table, Source column). Codes the body never uses; the reader must reverse-engineer them from the Sources list.
- **"Chapter 5's price list"** (§4), **"Chapter 6's badge rule"** (§6), **"Chapter 6's forking paths"** (§7), **"Chapter 6's holdout"** (§8), **"Chapter 6's three rungs"** (§8), **"Chapter 4's outlier-as-question"**, **"the positive-deviance search Chapter 4 opened and Chapter 6 could not source"** (§5). Each is a load-bearing cross-reference with no content given here; the chapter assumes the earlier chapters are in memory.
- **"Fixed (sequence)"** vs **"Fixed (requirement)"** vs **"Fixed (declared)"** (Truths and rules table). Three sub-tiers of Fixed are used; none is defined in the chapter and the difference between them is not inferable.
- **"is a Reading"**, **"a Nightlife Culture position"**, **"Standard"**, **"Evidence"**, **"Practice"** as kinds. Deferred to Chapter 0 ("each carrying what Chapter 0 calls its kind"); the chapter itself gives no key, so the Truths' *Kind* lines read as code.
- **"the house's grounds"** (rules table, ranking row). Who is "the house"? Presumably Nightlife Culture, but it is never said.
- **"Identity measures … condition measures are compared through the gate"** (rules table; §13 "Identity measures … are never on an ordered axis"). The identity/condition distinction and "the gate" are both used as settled terms and neither is defined here; the Sources list hints at a "comparability gate" in a Style Guide I do not have.
- **"Version 1 of this book"** (§3, §4, rules table, Sources). A prior edition is cited, corrected and credited without saying what it was or where it stands.
- **"Version 1's split, extended and its examples corrected by this chapter"** (rules table, identity row). The chapter does not show the examples or the corrections; the row's Why column points at work that is not in the text.
- **"The Magenta Book's own feasibility table is the decision aid"** (§12). Named as the decision aid, not reproduced or summarised.
- **"the deviation entry Chapter 16 describes"** and **"the Decision Book has no rules on …"**. Two house documents invoked as if the reader holds them.
- **"the Cochrane group's floor"** (§7). Which Cochrane group, and why its admissibility floor matters to a dashboard, is assumed.
- **"a clinic's rank can move substantially"** (60-second version) against **"Many centres had substantial changes in rank"** (§3). Not a stall exactly, but the compressed version adds "substantially" to the rank movement where the source quote attaches "significantly" to the birth rate; a reader checking the two will pause.

## 5. Best passage

"A filled record, for an imagined comparison, so that the shape is clear." (§11). Ten abstract lines become one concrete district with thirty-four venues and three closures, and the sentence at the end — "Seven of the ten lines change what the number means" — made the whole chapter land in a way the rules table did not.

## 6. Verdict

A research partner could use it as it stands and would be grateful for it; a creative technologist could build from the rules table and the filled record but would trip on the undefined kinds, tiers, "the gate" and "identity measures"; a funder's analyst would get the argument and the ladder but would need the Magenta Book, Green Book, Vibe Map and "the studio" glossed before trusting that the chapter was written for them rather than about them.
