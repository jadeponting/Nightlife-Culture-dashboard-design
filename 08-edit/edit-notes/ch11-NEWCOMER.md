# Chapter 11 — newcomer read

## 1. What it teaches

The chapter decides that a public dashboard's first screen is a single sentence to a named reader that works before anyone touches anything, that every interaction must say what it buys and what it costs, and that any state a reader reaches must be citable as a dated, unchanging snapshot with a canonical address. It is written for whoever designs or commissions a public data display, with maps singled out as the hardest case (rates not counts, fixed breaks, small areas made visible). After reading it I would write the headline sentence before drawing anything, design for a phone first, put a table beside every map, and stop trusting a URL as a citation.

## 2. One rule, and why it exists

"Content on hover meets the three conditions (dismissible, hoverable, persistent) and is also reachable by keyboard focus; nothing is only on hover, because touch has no hover." The chapter's own evidence for it is in three parts: the W3C standard lists why hover content fails (accidental triggering, content appearing unnoticed, magnification following the pointer, users needing more time); the practitioners add that tooltips "are not normally available on touchscreens"; and the device figures say over half to two-thirds of the ONS's readers arrive on a phone or tablet, where there is no hover at all. If the value lives only in a tooltip, most readers never see it. The ONS's own chart components, which make tooltips keyboard-reachable and spoken, are offered as proof the rule is buildable.

## 3. Where I stalled

- "the three nothings, a felt distribution" (The question, first paragraph). Both terms arrive in the opening question with no gloss. "The three nothings" is not unpacked until section 8 (withheld, unmeasured, held), and the glossary at the end only says they are "Chapter 10's". "Felt distribution" / "felt measure" is never defined in this chapter at all.
- "The six landing patterns version 1 named" (60-second version). "Version 1" is used dozens of times as if the reader knows what it is. The only explanation is in the Sources list on the last page: "Version 1, Chapters 1, 9 and 10 (archived)". A newcomer reads most of the chapter unsure whether version 1 is an earlier draft of this book, a previous product, or a published paper.
- "the house's own shape" (section 4 opening), "the house rule", "the house's voice", "the house's crop test". "The house" is never introduced. I inferred from the rules table ("Nightlife Culture + Practice") that it means the studio, but the text never says so.
- "The book does not design the Vibe Map's first screen" (The question). First and almost only mention of the Vibe Map; nothing says what it is.
- "The brief's Chapter 11 asks how" (section 4) and "The brief's failure register names two failures" (section 9). Which brief? Not identified anywhere.
- "(abstract †)" (first used in Belief three, section 2) and "(read once)" (section 4). The dagger and the "read once" convention are only explained in the Sources preamble at the very end. Until then they read as stray footnote marks.
- "Fixed (requirement)", "Fixed (sequence)", "Adaptable (house default)" (rules table). The table header says "Kind and tier as in Chapter 0". The requirement/sequence distinction is not explained here, and I did not open Chapter 0, so I cannot tell what a "sequence" rule obliges me to do differently from a "requirement".
- "its cost is Lam's decision cost" (section 6, purchase list). Lam is named here for the first time; the cost framework two subsections earlier is described without naming its author, so the surname lands bare. The glossary later says "Lam's seven".
- "PECR's 'not about who uses it'" (section 6). Abbreviation never expanded.
- "ONS", "UKHSA", "UKSA", "NN/g", "DSH-12", "STM", "CRC". None expanded in the body. "ONS" and "the Analysis Function" carry the whole chapter and a non-UK reader, or a UK reader outside government statistics, will not know what either is or that one sits inside the other.
- "1.4.13's three conditions and its reasons; 2.5.1, 2.5.7, 2.5.8" (truth 4, section 10). The WCAG success-criterion numbers appear here bare. Section 6 describes the same criteria by name and level but never attaches the numbers, so the truth cannot be mapped back to the argument without the rules table.
- "the table twin" (section 6, target size; section 8, maps), "figures-before-script" (section 6, design systems), "the walk (Chapter 10)" (face sheet line 12), "Chapter 10's table builder" (purchase list), "Chapter 9's human dot plot" (purchase list), "Chapter 6's discipline … recipe, base, boundary" (section 5). Each is a term of art from another chapter, used as if already shared. The table twin I could guess; the walk and the table builder I could not.
- "provenance set, recipe card, claim record, comparison record, service record, measure record, safeguards record" (face sheet line 3) and "claim rung chip" (filled example). The seven records are listed by name only. A reader of this chapter alone has no idea what any contains.
- "Three-phones test run with one reader from each group" (filled example, line 12) is used before "the two-readers test" and "the three-phones test" are defined (rules table and "What proves a break works"). The checklist then calls it the "three-user-group phone test", a third name. "Each group" and "the three user groups" are never named; the text says "(Chapter 2)".
- "What is known is from section 8 and Chapter 8: a map is the costliest first screen to load (over two-fifths of the script budget for the renderer)" (section 3, map-first). Section 8 says nothing about load cost or a script budget; that claim belongs to Chapter 8 alone. The internal reference does not deliver.
- "the shape sections 3 and 8 describe" (section 3, UKHSA case: "message-first with figures, a stamp beside each, and a path"). Section 8 is about the data layer and mentions the stamp in passing; it does not describe a landing shape. Reads as a misdirected pointer.
- "a status in text (Chapter 4's code; the design system's chip)" (section 8). Everywhere else in the chapter "the design system" means GOV.UK, the NHS or the ONS. Here it appears to mean the studio's own design system. The sentence reads two ways.
- "The prototype's test is the three nothings side by side on one face" (section 8). Which prototype? Not introduced.
- "a stem the reader must drink through *costs* exploration" (section 4, the null). The Martini Glass image is a page earlier, so "stem" is recoverable, but on first read "drink through a stem" is a puzzle.
- "the ONS's own semi-automated area pages" (Belief one, section 2). Referenced as known; explained three sections later in section 5.
- "The ten-word chart-title rule version 1 quoted is no longer on the ONS's pages; fifteen is" (section 3). Grammatically it works, but "fifteen is" takes two reads, and the sentence is only meaningful to someone who knows what version 1 said.
- "on which the paper's abstract and body disagree" (section 9, about the onboarding study). This is the first the reader hears of a disagreement; section 6 only marked it "(read once)".
- "credibility d = 0.0, quality d = 0.5, readability d = 2.8" (section 5). Cohen's d is not named or glossed; a funder's analyst may not know that 2.8 is enormous.
- "The 60-second version" runs to roughly 1,300 words of dense quotation. The heading promises something it does not deliver; it is a ten-minute version.
- "Level AA", "Level A" (section 6). WCAG conformance levels are used without saying what the ladder is.
- "Bogost" (section 4, rhetoric). Bare surname, no first name or work.
- "scrollytelling" and "stepper versus scroller" (section 4, Flow). Neither is defined in the body or the glossary; "the practitioners who made the form" is not identified in the text (The Pudding, per Sources).

## 4. What reads like it was written for the people who made it, not for me

- "The book does not design the Vibe Map's first screen." (The question.) Addressed to people who know the project.
- "Version 1's '4 percentage points' is not the paper's figure; 4.08 is, and the only 'percentage points' on the page is a different comparison" (section 3, prediction). An erratum against an earlier draft.
- "The ten-word chart-title rule version 1 quoted is no longer on the ONS's pages; fifteen is." (section 3.)
- "The sentence version 1 attributed to the paper — that defaults are a form of procedural rhetoric — is not in it; the category and the default-view sentence are, and the reading that joins them is the book's." (section 4.)
- The whole subsection "The null that could not be re-read" (section 4): "Version 1 rested its sequencing rule on three field experiments … which this chapter withdraws, because the page they came from could not be reached. For this chapter the paper's full text could not be opened by any route — its open repository sits behind a bot wall; the publisher refused". This is a research log, not a chapter.
- "Per-cell numbers and effect sizes were not reachable." (section 4, linking and layout.)
- "Chapter 3's summary of this study — engagement up, comprehension flat — is corrected here on the first half." (section 4, Flow.) Cross-draft correction.
- "Version 1 said the authors called the taxonomy descriptive and not prescriptive; they do not use those words." (section 6.)
- "whether its countries are drawn before any rating could not be established" and "(the two figures could not be reconciled)" (section 3, select-first).
- "could be read only at its abstract, and its findings are not stated here from memory (abstract †)" (section 8, missing data).
- "(abstract †)", "(read once)", "(secondary)", "(reconstructed)" scattered through every section, and the Sources preamble that explains them: "a dagger (†) marks a source read at the authors' abstract only, 'secondary' a quotation reached through a named reproduction …". Verification apparatus on the face of the text.
- "It is carried as a bet, recorded as one, and the shape stands on the three that were read." (section 4.)
- "The prototype's test is the three nothings side by side on one face, tried with readers" (section 8). A project plan.
- "the only evidence the studio will have of whether the failure has occurred" (section 9).
- "The excuses it lists are the ones a studio will hear from itself" (section 7).
- "the house's data contract — the repository's own rules for its data" (section 8).
- The whole of section 11, "Where the choice is yours", which is a list of decisions awaiting the studio: "Which the Vibe Map uses, and whether a generated sentence is held to the poster rule, is the studio's."; "which kinds, in what order, and how far below the sentence, is the studio's"; "the studio decides when and how"; "the chapter's view is that publishing it would be the first public evidence of its kind".
- "The example is a shape and not a plan; its sentence, its districts and its parameters are placeholders." (section 10, after the filled face sheet.)
- Section 12, "A nightlife illustration — method only": "A studio has a sentence for a region and wants a first screen." Written for the studio's own next step.
- "What the field does not know", bullet one: "The storytelling null — version 1's load-bearing evidence for 'the story is never a gate' — could not be re-read at source, and its two figures are not in the reachable abstract; the chapter carries the direction as secondary … withdraws the figures".
- Rules table sources: "Version 1 audit"; "Version 1 crop test"; "Version 1 Ch 10; DSH-12"; "Decision Book Ch 7"; "Decision Book Ch 15". Internal document codes with no key.
- Sources: "Version 1's figures withdrawn pending confirmation"; "Read in the repository"; "The Decision Book, Chapters 7 and 15 and the rule register; Style Guide v1.5, dashboard section (DSH-01–21)"; and the "Verified." / "Spot-checked." tags on every entry.
- No sentence names Carly, agents, tools or sprints.

## 5. Best passage

"The largest direct test. Two crowdsourced experiments, 530 and 2,149 recruited …" (section 6). It taught me the most because it ends by splitting the two sentences a dashboard's analytics will blur: "People who interact do better; interaction does not make people do better."

## 6. Verdict

A research partner could use it as it stands, with patience; a creative technologist could work from the rules table, the face sheet and the checklist and skip the body; a funder's analyst could not, because the argument leans on an undefined house vocabulary (version 1, the house, the seven records, the table twin, the walk), on cross-chapter references that are not always honoured, and on a running commentary about what earlier drafts got wrong and what could not be downloaded.
