# Chapter 10 — newcomer read

## 1. What it teaches

The chapter decides that a public dashboard about communities faces two separate problems with two separate toolkits: *exposing* is a disclosure problem (floors, suppression, rounding, perturbation, the motivated intruder, a filter treated as a table builder), and *exploiting* is a consent-and-voice problem for which the law protects persons and nothing protects groups or places, so the dashboard must build its own protections and write them down in a fourteen-line safeguards record. It is written for anyone putting people on a public dashboard, and most pointedly for a small private studio that is not a university, not a public body and not a member of any ethics code, and must therefore adopt its stances deliberately. After reading it I would write the record's first line before collecting anything, model the intruder as a regular who knows the venues rather than a stranger with a national rule, never say "the community consented" but record who was consulted, who governs and who stood at the door, and declare a "known but not shown" status for a category of place rather than flag the individual room.

## 2. One rule, and why it exists

"The held layer is a declared status for a category, never a marker on a unit: present in a coarser count, decided by a named process, reason public, review date set." The chapter's own evidence for it is in section 14: a marker on a single room saying *held at the request of the people in it* tells every onlooker that this room has something to protect, so the holding publishes the inference it was meant to prevent. The census office keeps its perturbation parameters private for the parallel reason that knowledge of them "may increase confidence in apparent attribute disclosures". Every precedent the chapter found does it the category way: eBird shows sensitive species "at grid-cell level (400 km2 accuracy)" and declares the species list; the NBN Atlas holds a list "agreed by the UK's statutory bodies" so that partners "do not have to make their own assessments"; the environmental regulations run a presumption of openness, a named harm and a weighing. None hides the hiding and none marks the hidden unit.

## 3. Where I stalled

- **"The brief's phrase has two verbs"** (60-second version, first line). What brief? It is never introduced; by section 1 I gathered it is the commissioning document for the dashboard, and only the Sources list names it ("BRIEF-v2"). "The brief's failure register" (section 1) is likewise assumed.
- **"the house does not use consent"** / **"Nightlife Culture rule, Adaptable (house default)"**. "The house" and "the studio" are used interchangeably from the 60-second version onward and never explained. In the rules table "Nightlife Culture" appears as a *kind* of source alongside "Standard" and "Evidence", which a newcomer cannot parse until the Sources section reveals it is the studio's own name.
- **"the small room is shown as present"** (60-second version). "Room" meaning a venue or a scene's space is studio vocabulary; nothing says so until section 5 ("a room with twelve answers") and the glossary at the end never defines it.
- **"the felt layer"** (subtitle), **"a felt measure"**, **"the money view"**. Three recurring objects, none defined in this chapter. "Money view" especially: by section 15 it is clearly a panel about funding or investment, but I had to infer it.
- **"the measure record's line 12"** / **"line 5 — who answered and who did not"** (sections 13, 16). A record from another chapter cited by line number; the reader has no idea what line 12 holds. Same for **"the provenance set, the recipe card, the claim record, the comparison record, the service record"** in section 18, six names with no gloss.
- **"Chapter 6's reasoning that a community's own regulars could name the venues behind a count of three"** (60-second version). Leans on another chapter's finding as if known; it is restated later in section 4, but the first use is bare.
- **"Chapter 4 named six kinds of nothing"** (section 14). The chapter then distinguishes "three of Chapter 4's six kinds" without saying what the other three are, so the reader cannot judge whether the three nothings here are the right three.
- **"a friendly intruder's walk before release"** (60-second version). "The walk" is used as a fixed term from the top; intruder testing is only explained in section 7.
- **"the layer is either lit or coarsened further"**, **"before it is lit"**, **"a money view is lit for a scene"**. "Lit" is never defined; I read it as "switched on / published", but it reads two ways next to "held".
- **"never the face"**, **"on the face"**, **"a reader meets on a face"**. "The face" for the dashboard's visible surface is house usage, not defined.
- **"a table twin for every map (Chapter 8)"** / **"accessible twin"**. Undefined; presumably a data table beside each map, but the reader guesses.
- **"Fixed (sequence)"** vs **"Fixed (requirement)"** vs **"Adaptable (house default)"**. The tiers are pointed at Chapter 0 ("Kind and tier as in Chapter 0") and the sequence/requirement distinction is never explained here. In section 19, "The procedure is Fixed; the number is the studio's" uses the capitalised tier in running prose.
- **"Standard + Reading"**, **"Practice + Reading"** (rules table). "Reading" as a kind of source is unexplained.
- **"(abstract only †)"**, **"(read once)"**. The dagger and "read once" appear from the 60-second version and section 3 onward, dozens of times, and are only decoded in the preamble to Sources at the very end. A reader meets forty-odd of them before learning what they mean.
- **"a DPIA is required for"** (60-second version). Bare abbreviation; expanded only in section 9. **"PECR"** (section 9, rules) is never expanded. **"UKSA"**, **"OSR"**, **"EIR 2004"**, **"OSA s.35"**, **"QMI"**, **"EAP168"** appear only in the rules table and sources. **"the DAS systematically undercounts"** (section 6) is quoted without saying DAS is the census Disclosure Avoidance System; **"the HOLC maps"** (section 15) is quoted without expansion; **"If DP or perturbation used"** (checklist) assumes DP.
- **"The house's history with a policing form (section 8)"** (section 4). Section 8 then says the form "is Carly's knowledge, enters this chapter as frame, and is not researched here". The cross-reference promises an explanation and delivers a disclaimer; the reader never learns what the form was or why it matters, yet it is given as the reason for a rule.
- **"the chapter's floors apply to venues as to persons. Data-protection law protects natural persons"** (section 5). This paragraph says floors cover venues, then that companies are not persons in law, then that the statistics code covers businesses and that is the standard adopted, then that defamation was not researched. Four turns in five sentences; I had to read it three times to see it was not contradicting itself.
- **"Three is a static-table convention"** (section 19). Where three comes from is in Chapter 6, not here.
- **"version 1's filter walk"** (rules table source). Unexplained.
- **"the extraction question, answered with evidence"** (line 14) and **"consequences as validity evidence"** (rules table). Both lean on Chapter 9 material not restated.
- **"Section 8's intention test (the chapter's section 8, not the Regulation's Article 8A)"**. Self-correcting, but the fact it needs the parenthesis shows the numbering collides.
- **"Chapter 11 carries the display question"** (section 14) and **"whether they are one document is Chapter 16's question"** (section 18). Forward references a reader of this chapter cannot check.

## 5. Best passage

"The third thing an intruder can learn is not about a person at all." (section 3, Group). It turns the whole chapter: a table that identifies nobody can still say a district is poor or a venue's regulars are mostly of one ethnicity, the regulator calls that fairness rather than disclosure, the toolkit has nothing for it, and the dashboard that clusters venues into scenes is assembling exactly the "ad hoc group" whose members were never asked.

## 6. Verdict

A research partner could use it as it stands and would be grateful for it; a creative technologist could build from sections 5, 7, 14 and the record if given a one-page glossary of the house words (face, lit, held, room, felt measure, money view, twin, the brief, the tiers); a funder's analyst would get the 60-second version and the rules table and bounce off the rest, because the verification apparatus, the chapter cross-references and the "for counsel" and "for the studio" deferrals make it read as a working file for the people building the thing rather than a finished chapter.
