# Chapter 14 — newcomer read

## 1. What it teaches

The chapter decides that a public dashboard has two layers, an evidence layer that must say all and only what the data says and an expressive layer laid on top of it, and that the line between them is checked by stripping the expressive layer away (greyscale, muted, motion off, styling removed) and seeing whether the facts survive and whether anything false was added. It is written for whoever designs or signs off the look of a public data display, and it tells them what the research lets a styling choice claim (engagement, mood, recognition, memorability) and what it does not (accuracy, empathy, action). After reading it I would stop defending a chart's trustworthiness by its plainness, I would ship a light version beside any dark one, and I would write down for every decorative element what it is for and what happened when it was removed.

## 2. One rule, and why it exists

"Mood is carried only in channels that encode nothing." The chapter's reason is Blair, Wang and Perin (2024): across five crowdsourced experiments, colour palette moved readers' feelings more than the trend in the data did. If a palette chosen for mood is laid on a scale that carries a quantity, the reader's feeling about the number is being set by the colour and not by the number, which is a fact the data does not say. Bartram (2017) adds that mood is carried by lightness and chroma, so it can be carried in type, ground and ornament instead, with the data's own channels left plain.

## 3. Where I stalled

- "The house's three tiers" (60-second version, first paragraph). "The house", "Fixed", "Adaptable" and "Decorative" are used as settled vocabulary before any of them is explained; section 3 half-explains them a long way down, and "the house" is never explained at all.
- "(read once)" appears from the first paragraph of the 60-second version and recurs more than a hundred times, with "†" alongside. Neither is explained until the Sources section at the very end. For most of the chapter they read as noise or as a private marking.
- "a different claim from the twin's" (60-second version). "The twin" and "the precise twin" are used throughout; the definition arrives in "Words this chapter uses" after the rules table.
- "from Chapter 3's line" and "Chapter 2's persuasion line". "Line" meaning a strand of evidence is a house usage I had to infer.
- "The Style Guide's evidence standard" and "the Decision Book" (60-second version, section 2 onwards). Which style guide, whose decision book? Only the Sources list says they are Nightlife Culture's documents. "BRK-07" and "Decision Book Ch 18" are bare codes.
- "Version 1 made the case, in its Chapter 13". Never explained what Version 1 is. Worse, "Version 1's Chapter 13" and "Chapter 13" (this book's floor chapter) are both cited repeatedly, and "the Decision Book's Chapter 7" sits beside "Chapter 7" (the frame). Three numbering systems, no key.
- "the committee export" (section 6, Undressed). Used as the name of the stripped page before it is defined in Words.
- "What the failure register asks of this chapter" (section 1) and "The brief's register" (section 9). I could not tell what the failure register is or where it comes from until section 9 quotes items from it; "the brief" is never explained.
- "Nothing here decides the Vibe Map's palette" (section 1). The Vibe Map is never introduced. A reader outside the project does not know whether it is a product, a plan or a place.
- "with its unit, base, date and gate" (sections 6, 10, 12, checklist). "Gate" is never explained. Unit, base and date I can guess; gate I cannot.
- "the three user groups Chapter 0 names"; "the cut-out rule"; "the message-before-input rule"; "the identity line"; "the floor"; "the service record"; "the provenance set"; "the recipe card"; "the claim record". Each is attributed to a chapter and none is glossed. The gallery section leans on five of them in one paragraph.
- "the two-audience claim, which has no measurement anywhere in this book" (section 11 and What the field does not know). What the two-audience claim is, I never found out.
- "the house's probes" (sections 7, 8, 10, rules). What a probe is in this house is not said; I inferred a user-testing round.
- "*(Standard (method).)*", "*(Evidence.)*", "*(Reading.)*", "*(Practice.)*", "*(Nightlife Culture; Reading.)*". Paragraph-end tags in italics throughout, never explained in this chapter. I guessed they classify the kind of source; "Reading" versus "Nightlife Culture" I could not separate.
- "Fixed (requirement)" versus "Fixed (sequence)" in the rules table. The bracketed distinction is not explained.
- "Expression is measured to buy engagement" (60-second version, truth 2, section 5). The phrase "measured to buy" reads two ways on first pass: measured in order to buy, or measured as buying. It is the second, and the sentence has to be reread to get there.
- "which Mackinlay calls 'conjectural' and, beyond quantitative data, 'not been empirically verified'" (60-second version). The quoted fragment does not fit the sentence's grammar and the clause had to be reread.
- "null results for disfluency, WMC, and their interaction" (section 5). WMC left bare; it is working memory capacity.
- "the uppercase words had higher mean LIs than the lower" (section 5). LI is not introduced; "legibility index" is defined two paragraphs later.
- "AF infographics; AF §10" in the rules table. The Analysis Function is never abbreviated in the body, only here.
- "d = 2.17", "probability of superiority of about 0.54", "H1-1-1", "(H1-3-3)". Effect sizes and hypothesis codes printed bare for a reader who may not read statistics.
- "cited in the log only" (Sources, Lazard & Atkinson; Li & Moacdieh). Which log? Neither paper appears in the body, and the boundary log is a design record, not a reading record.
- "Kostelnick 2008" appears in Sources and in the preamble as unreadable but nowhere in the body.

## 5. Best passage

"The test the Decision Book names ... has a step before it that the four words leave implicit: the sort." Section 6's first paragraph. It turns a slogan into a procedure with two different outcomes (a lost fact means the sort was wrong; an added fact means a breach), and the DWP two-houses case a few paragraphs later shows the second outcome happening to a real government graphic.

## 6. Verdict

A research partner could use sections 3 to 6, 8 and the rules table with effort, but a creative technologist or a funder's analyst would stall within the first page on house vocabulary, three overlapping chapter numberings, unexplained verification marks and parked decisions, so as it stands the chapter is usable by the team that wrote it and not yet by anyone else.
