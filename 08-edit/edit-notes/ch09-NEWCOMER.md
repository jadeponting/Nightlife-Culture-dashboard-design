# Chapter 9 — Newcomer read

Read in full (760 lines), knowing nothing else about the project. No other file opened.

## 1. What it teaches

The chapter decides that a "vibe" is three different things (made, perceived, in excess of both), that only the perceived one has a tested way of being asked, and that any felt measure of a place is a spread of people's answers with its sample and gaps on its face, never a score. It is written for whoever is about to put a felt layer on a dashboard about places at night, and for the sceptic who will ask that person what the number is evidence of. After reading it I would never show a mean, a heat map, a check-in count or a sentiment score as "how the place feels"; I would open the twelve-line measure record before writing a single question, define the place to the respondent in the question, and plan from day one how the people who answered will be told what their answers changed.

## 2. One rule, and why it exists

**"A felt result is reported as a distribution with bands, never as a point alone; coordinates or scores are computed per respondent, not from the room's median."** The chapter grounds this twice. The soundscape protocol's own analysts compute the two coordinates "for each individual response" rather than taking the median answer per attribute and then plotting it, and argue for speaking of the "percent of people likely to perceive" a place one way, because a single average hides who disagreed. The national wellbeing programme reached the same conclusion from the other direction: it reports its four 0–10 questions as bands as well as means because "relying only on average ratings might mask important differences in how people rate their well-being", and it set its mid-point cut where cognitive testing found respondents themselves treat the scale as turning negative. Two unrelated fields, one in acoustics and one in official statistics, independently refused the point and kept the spread. That is why the rule is Fixed.

## 5. Best passage

"Two things follow that a builder should see before borrowing the word." (§3.) The arithmetic that shows "vibrant" has no coordinate of its own, lands on both axes, can be reached by a place where few people endorsed the item, and sits on the one axis respondents read unevenly. I understood the whole chapter's caution from that paragraph.

## 6. Verdict

A research partner could use it as it stands and would trust the sourcing; a creative technologist or a funder's analyst would get the seven truths, the measure record and the rules table, but would stall on the unexplained scaffolding (the brief, Version 1, the house framework, the other chapters' five records, the Fixed/Adaptable tiers), on four source markers defined only on line 615, and on a research log of closed PDFs running through the body, so: usable with a glossary and a trim, not as it stands.
