# Chapter 15 — Newcomer read

## 1. What it teaches

The chapter decides that a public dashboard about a place's culture should borrow from public health, finance, place statistics, the charity sector and the big open-data projects only those practices whose rule is printed, whose reason is stated, and whose evidence or cost is known, and it names seven of these as non-negotiable: trust measured by a fixed repeated question or not at all, a documentation page per metric plus a labelled change log, no arrow or verdict-colour without a printed significance rule, event-dated series with vintages kept, uncertainty as a range not a hedge, a "can and cannot say" text on any ranking, and a written retirement plan before launch. It is written for whoever will build and run such a dashboard and for whoever will review it. After reading it I would stop reporting pageviews as "reach", refuse to draw a trend arrow on a young series, and keep every data file I ever published, dated, from day one.

## 2. One rule, and why it exists

"Keep every published data file as a dated vintage from the first release." The chapter's evidence is Croushore and Stark's demonstration that re-running a classic economic test with later, revised data makes "the coefficients change dramatically", and the St Louis Fed's ALFRED archive built so that analysts can work "with data that were available at the time". The reason the rule is Fixed rather than Adaptable is the clause "it cannot be started later": a vintage archive begun in year three has no year one, so the choice has to be made before the first release.

## 3. Where I stalled

- "the failure register" (section 1, and heading of section 10). Used as a known object from the first page and never defined in this chapter. Section 10 quotes eight failures in quotation marks ("It fails if it looks the same every month") without saying whose words they are or where the register lives. I inferred it is a house document from an earlier chapter, but I had to infer it.
- "the house" (throughout, from section 1). Never defined. I eventually worked out from context that it means the studio building the dashboard, but a reader who does not know the book's convention will read "what the house changes" in the precedent-record table as a lodging or a betting term.
- "the Vibe Map" (section 1, "it does not decide what the Vibe Map borrows"). Used as a known name with no gloss anywhere in the chapter. I gather it is the dashboard being built, but the chapter never says so.
- "recipe card, service record, comparison record, face sheet, boundary log" (section 11, precedent-record table, and section 13). Five record names, each attributed to another chapter by number (5, 8, 11), none explained here. The sentence "A borrowed rule lives in the record it serves" is unusable without knowing what those records are.
- "this one leaves the twelfth" (section 11). I cannot count the other eleven from this chapter, and "like the boundary log it grows by one entry" compares to a thing I have not met.
- "Chapter 12's identity line" (section 9, "What the chapter would not carry over"). A ranked top-ten table is declined on the strength of a line in another chapter that is not quoted or paraphrased.
- "(read once)", "(two passes)", "(fragment verified)", "†" (throughout, from the 60-second version onward). The dagger is explained only in the Sources section at the very end ("A figure or quotation taken from an abstract or a secondary account carries †"), and "two passes" and "fragment verified" are not in the status key at all. A reader meets dozens of these tags before reaching the key. The parenthetical "(the remainder of the sentence read once)" in section 4 is a verification note that reads as part of the quotation.
- "*(Evidence; Practice.)*", "*(Reading.)*", "*(Standard (official statistics).)*" (end of nearly every paragraph). These italic kind-labels are never introduced. "Reading" in particular I had to guess at: it seems to mean "the chapter's own inference" but could equally mean "a reading recommendation".
- "Fixed (requirement)" versus "Fixed (sequence)" versus "Adaptable" (rules table, Tier column). The tiers are not explained in this chapter. "Fixed (sequence)" I took to mean "must happen before launch", but that is a guess.
- "Section 3 and section 7" cited under the first belief in section 2 as where producers' trust is measured; the PCOS material is in section 7 but the Charity Commission tracker is in section 6, which is not cited. Minor, but the pointer is incomplete.
- "Chapter 5's funnel discipline rests on" (section 3, Fingertips). Promised explanation is in another chapter; here it is only asserted that the funnel-plot warning is "Chapter 5's funnel discipline, in its home sector's words".
- "PCOS", "ELS QMI", "OSR", "ESRB", "FPC", "CTP", "TAA", "IoD", "GII", "SMS", "RWB", "NPO", "ACE", "UKSA", "GSS" (rules table Source column and Sources section). Most are expanded somewhere in the body, but the rules table uses them bare, and "ELS QMI", "SMS" (Scientific Maryland Scale) and "RWB" are never expanded where they appear in the table. "CC platform" in section 6 (Nordicity quote) is never expanded: Culture Counts, presumably, but the reader has to join it to a later sentence.
- "the chapter corrects its own brief: the 'twice as deprived' rule it expected is not in the FAQ" (section 5). Reads two ways: either the brief was wrong and the chapter is noting it, or the chapter is apologising for a prior draft. Either way a newcomer does not know what "its own brief" is.
- "Chapter 12's peer method now has an official specification to cite" (section 5, ONS similar-areas). A promise about another chapter that this one does not deliver.
- "the one 'can'" (section 5, deprivation indices). The sentence "and the one 'can': comparison across editions 'does allow some comparisons…'" reads oddly: the "one can" is an inverted-commas device for a permitted use, but it took a second read.
- "Fingertips' amber rule is narrower than first read" (What the field does not know). "First read" by whom? This is a note about the authors' own earlier misreading, and a newcomer cannot tell what the earlier reading was.
- "with a false control rejected" (Sources, UKHSA compliance page). A verification-method term, unexplained.
- Section 3 heading "What was measured about the dashboards" then "*The readers, once.*" The italic run-in heads ("The pages", "The teams", "The readers, once", "Two experiments") are a device, but "once" is ambiguous: once in time, or the only study of readers?

## 4. What reads like it was written for the people who made it, not for me

- "This chapter exists because 'take inspiration from the best in the world' was said at the start." (section 1) — refers to an unquoted brief and an unnamed speaker.
- "So this chapter has a test to pass before it has anything to teach... does the chapter find enough that Chapters 2 and 5 did not to earn its place as a chapter rather than a reading list?" (The question; section 1; "What the chapter adds to Chapters 2 and 5") — the chapter arguing for its own inclusion in a book is an editorial conversation, not reader content.
- "It found about forty, listed in section 9; about six were already in the book in another form." (section 1) — a drafting tally.
- "and the chapter corrects its own brief: the 'twice as deprived' rule it expected is not in the FAQ" (section 5); and "(Three things the chapter's own brief expected were not found: ...)" (section 6); and "The Culture and Heritage Capital multiplier warning the brief expected. Reported absent" (What the field does not know) — three places where the chapter reports on the gap between its commissioning brief and what it found. That is a research-log note, not teaching.
- "(re-checked at source for this chapter)", "(returned identically on three passes and re-checked at source for this chapter)", "(confirmed at source for the cases page)", "(two rows confirmed twice: ... the rest must be read from the PDF by eye)", "(fragment verified; sentence read once)", "(the remainder of the sentence read once)", "a pass listing every fan-chart sentence in it found none" — verification process narrated inline throughout sections 3 to 7.
- "(the brief's 2019 citation is the *Journal of Monetary Economics* version, 108, 1–15, with four authors)" (section 4) — a bibliographic correction to the brief, addressed to whoever wrote the brief.
- "(The abstract's 'color-coding was common (93/158, 89.4%)' is a slip; the body's consistent figure is 93 of the 104 dashboards that used visual cues.)" (section 3) — a fact-check note that belongs in a log.
- "which this book's own dagger convention meets in Chapter 16" (section 4) — a note about the book's house style compliance.
- "Fingertips' amber rule is narrower than first read: amber when a leave-one-out trend runs the opposite way. Stated as such." (What the field does not know) — a note about the authors' own revision between drafts.
- "Statuses: *verified* = quoted text confirmed by presence re-fetch at the URL on 6 October 2026; *read once* = returned once and not re-checked..." and the whole Sources section's per-item status strings ("*Verified*; items marked read once where so"; "*Read once* (figures returned on verification pass)"; "(cited in the log only)") — a verification audit, including references to "the log" that the reader does not have.
- "Compliance page re-checked at source for this chapter (presence check, with a false control rejected)." (Sources) — method of verification, for the verifiers.
- "BRIEF-v2 §6 and §7 (Chapter 15's definition). Version 1, Chapter 12 (archived)." (Sources, House) — internal document names and version history.
- "The precedent record in section 11 is where each decision would be written"; "section 12 says which decisions are the studio's" (section 1); the whole of section 12, "Where the choice is yours": "is the studio's, with the precedent record as the place the choice is written"; "Whether to ask it, of whom, and how often, is the studio's"; "the studio decides their content — what the Vibe Map's archive, licence, successor, notice period and gap analysis would be"; "How to use any of them is the studio's decision" — the section is addressed to the studio as a party that still has to decide things, which is a handover note rather than a chapter.
- "Which of section 9's mechanisms beyond the above to adopt, and when their conditions are met | The offer, with terms | — | Adaptable | Section 9" (last row of the rules table) — a rule that is a pending decision.
- "A filled example, invented, for method only... The example decides nothing about the Vibe Map's data" (section 11) and "Nothing above names the series, the place, the window's length, the colour or the words. It is the procedure by which the studio decides them" (section 13) — repeated disclaimers that the chapter is not deciding, which signal an unresolved boundary between the writers and a decision-maker.
- "It is not research into nightlife, in any form." (section 1) — a scope defence addressed to whoever set the scope.
- "The sectors did not run this test; the house can." (section 13) — an instruction to the house.
- "owner, the data lead" (section 11, filled example) — names a role in the studio.
- "Chapter 6's audience yardstick is orphaned, and the continuity arrangement is one sentence of 'our understanding'." (section 6) — reads as a note to the authors of Chapter 6 that their source has failed.

No sentence names Carly or waits on her directly.

## 5. Best passage

"A series of events that arrive late has two dates..." (section 3, "The two dates, and the tail that always looks like a fall"). It states a problem I had never named, gives the sector's answer in four parts, and ends with a rule I could apply tomorrow.

## 6. Verdict

A research partner could use it as it stands, with effort; a creative technologist or a funder's analyst would be stopped within the first two pages by undefined house terms ("the house", "the failure register", "the Vibe Map", the eleven records, the kind-labels and tier names) and slowed throughout by the inline verification tags, so as it stands it is usable only by someone who has already read the rest of the book.
