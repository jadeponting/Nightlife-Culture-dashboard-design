# Chapter 2 — the newcomer read

Read in full: `04-chapters/ch02-who-reads-it-and-what-turns-their-decision.md` (621 lines), with `ch00-how-to-read-this-book.md` consulted for the tier legend, the five kinds, the dagger and the project nouns (Vibe Map, Nightlife Culture, the brief, the Style Guide). Anything ch00 explains is not listed as a stall below.

## 1. What it teaches

The chapter decides that a dashboard has no single reader: eight kinds of people will grade the same screen by rules that contradict each other, so the designer must name each reader, their decision, the evidence they are obliged to accept and the clock they keep, and then choose openly whose rules the first screen speaks. It is written for whoever is about to build or test a public-interest dashboard in the UK, and above all for the person who has to face a Treasury-trained officer, a councillor, a developer, a funder, a trade body and the scene itself with one display. After reading it I would stop trying to make a display persuade, label every panel as monitoring rather than evaluation, show the denominator and the caveats on the face, make sure a councillor can find their ward, and take the thing back to the people it describes before releasing it rather than after.

## 2. One rule, and why it exists

**"Label every panel as monitoring or as evaluation; never imply a counterfactual the display does not have."** The chapter's evidence is the Magenta Book's own figure: monitoring answers "Did we do what we said we would do? How are our success metrics changing over time?", evaluation answers "Did the intervention work as expected? What was the impact, on who, and why?". A dashboard can only do the first. The three evidence ladders in daily use then score the gap: a before-and-after is Maryland Level 1 and is "screened out of the review before it is read"; data showing change is Nesta Level 2, "you cannot confirm you caused this". So an implied causal claim is not a stretch the official reader forgives; it is the thing they are trained to discount, and the rule exists so the display never spends its credibility on a claim its grammar cannot make.

## 3. Where I stalled

- **"in five cases"** (60-second version). On first meeting I read "cases" as instances, not as the five sections of a business case. The 60-second version front-loads "cases", "ladder", "rung" and "grammar" before any of them is defined; the body explains them, but the summary is meant to stand alone.
- **"a comparison the dashboard can host but cannot supply"** (60-second version). I could not tell what "host" meant until section 12 ("the place to host the comparison, not to assert it"). The summary needs the half-sentence the end of the chapter gives it.
- **"a What Works Centre is involved"** (table, section 1). Used in the spine table before section 3 explains what a What Works Centre is. The paragraph above the table admits this and lists "section 106" among the terms "explained in the sections that follow"; section 106 is in fact explained only in the table row's own brackets, nowhere later.
- **"section 3's last part says why"** (section 3, the ladders). The sentence sits inside section 3 and refers to section 3 as if from outside. "The last part of this section" is what is meant.
- **"confirms the moderator"** (section 5, Markant). "Moderator" is a statistics term for a variable that changes the size of an effect; it is used here without introduction and only becomes clear at "Prior attitude is the master moderator" in the section's close.
- **"Amazon Mechanical Turk"** (section 5). Not explained. A reader outside research will not know this is a paid online panel, which matters for how much weight to give the Pandey numbers.
- **"44.5% against 53.2% saw it"** (section 5, maps). Which figure belongs to the counts map and which to the rates map is not said; the sentence reads two ways.
- **"†"** (first appears mid-prose in section 5, after Joslyn & LeClerc, then after Peters, Kahan, James & Moseley, Lowe, Smith-Merry, McIntosh & Wright, Birt). No key until the Sources preamble on line 500, which says the mark is used "in the rules table" — but it appears in body text eight times before that. Ch00 defines it; this chapter should at least point there at first use.
- **"NPC 2012, 2013; CEP"**, **"TNLCF; Esmée"** (truth 17), **"WWC Growth"** (rules table, twice), **"IfG 2020"**, **"UKSA 2017"** (rules table). Bare abbreviations. New Philanthropy Capital, the National Lottery Community Fund and the Institute for Government are named in full in the body but never with their initials; "CEP" (Center for Effective Philanthropy) and "WWC" appear only abbreviated.
- **"the economic-growth evidence centre"** (sections 6 and 7). The What Works Centre for Local Economic Growth is called, in turn, "the Centre", "What Works Growth", "WWC Growth", "the economic-growth evidence centre" and "the body whose job is to tell councils what grows a local economy". Five names for one body; I lost track of whether they were the same.
- **"two Treasury-sanctioned methods"** (section 6, headline-to-caveat decay). Wellbeing valuation and willingness to pay are called "Treasury-sanctioned" here, but section 3's account of the Green Book did not mention stated preference or willingness to pay, so the sanction is asserted rather than shown.
- **"Hold that beside Chapter 1's working definition"**, **"Chapter 1's truth 8"**, **"the Code of Practice Chapter 1 adopted"**, **"the clinical-dashboard review Chapter 1 relied on"**, **"the same three words as Chapter 1"**. Five places lean on Chapter 1 content without restating it. Ch00 gives the working definition, but not truth 8, the clinical review or the "three words". A reader starting here cannot follow these.
- **"the 2018 Cochrane review"**, **"Nesta's Standards"**, **"the Levelling Up missions"**. Cochrane, Nesta and Levelling Up are not glossed. Each is a UK or research-world proper noun that a creative technologist may not know.
- **"(Dr Foster was the company..."** (section 5, rankings). Dr Foster is introduced inside a parenthesis that explains why no effect is asserted for a thing nothing earlier mentioned. Reads as a note left in from research, not as prose for me.

## 4. What reads like it was written for the people who made it, not for me

- "All three reached this chapter through secondary sources and are marked accordingly." (section 4, Enlightenment)
- "These quotations are relayed by a reference summary (AcaWiki) and a 2012 National Research Council volume; the original article has not yet been read." (section 4, Weiss) — "not yet" waits on someone to read it.
- "(Wansink's wider body of work has since been heavily retracted; this paper's status was not checked here, and its own figures are not reproduced.)" (section 5)
- "a measured effect of that guide on patient choice was searched for and not found, so none is asserted." (section 5, Dr Foster)
- "The table is therefore the first thing to test in conversation with the real readers, not the last." (section 1) — asks for a piece of work to be done.
- "it should be treated as a hypothesis to test on real readers before it is treated as a rule" (section 5) and "a design choice with a thin evidential basis that the prototype can thicken" (section 11) — both wait on the prototype.
- "That is a trade, and it is a Nightlife Culture decision; what is Fixed is that it is made and shown" (section 11) and "choosing which is the studio's decision to make in the open" (section 12) — ask the studio to decide.
- "a plain-table view is a persuasion strategy with one study behind it, and the Vibe Map's first readers are the place to find out whether it holds." (section 12)
- "so the dialogue that changes the account rests on this chapter's reading, not on Birt's demonstration" (section 9) — an audit note on the chapter's own sourcing.
- Sources list, several entries: "chapters 1–5 only", "two targeted reads", "journal page blocked", "the journal text was not read", "Dimension statements not quoted (not verifiable from readable pages)", "Not read at source". Ch00 says the sources list records how each was read, so the convention is intended; but "journal page blocked" and "two targeted reads" describe the research session rather than the source.
- Rules table: "Style Guide v1.5" as a source; Sources: "The brief, version 2 — the failure register". Internal documents cited as if I had them. Ch00 names them, so this is a pointer problem rather than a stall, but a line in the rules table saying where to find them would help.

Citing Carly's brief and *Artists Make Great Places* as sources is fine throughout; nothing in the chapter asks Carly herself to do anything.

## 5. Best passage

"Hold that beside Chapter 1's working definition. A dashboard, in the official reader's grammar, is a monitoring object..." (section 3, Evaluation). It turns a Treasury figure into the one sentence that governs every later rule, and says plainly that this is not good dashboards against bad ones but what the word means to the person holding the money.

## 6. Verdict

Yes: a funder's analyst could use sections 3 and 7 and the rules table tomorrow, a research partner could use the whole chapter with the sources list as a reading plan, and a creative technologist could use sections 1, 5, 11 and 12 — provided the Chapter 1 dependencies, the bare abbreviations and the research-session asides are cleared, because those are the places where I, knowing nothing, had to stop.
