# Dashboard Design — the research book

**How dashboards work, and why — the methodological and theoretical foundation behind the Vibe Map's evidence displays.**

Nightlife Culture · Carly Heath · started 16 August 2026 · version 2 written 5–7 October 2026 · seventeen chapters at second draft

---

## What this is

A research book that teaches someone meeting the subject for the first time how evidence is turned into a display a reader can trust, read and act on — and what it costs when that is done badly. It is the third of the studio's foundations:

| Foundation | Answers | Lives |
|---|---|---|
| The Decision Book (`design-decisions`) | How to choose | `~/Documents/GitHub/decision-book` |
| The design system (`nightlife-culture-design`, Style Guide v1.5) | What the values are | `~/Documents/GitHub/Nightlife Culture Design System` |
| **This book** | **How evidence becomes a display that steers a decision** | here |

It serves the Vibe Map prototype — a living, interactive account of the people, activity, value and support systems that sustain a place's nightlife culture — but it is not an assessment, a specification or a design of it. The prototype's own records live in `vibe-map-prototype` and `vibe-map-ukdf-scope`.

A dashboard, in this book, is *a display that turns data into a decision for a named reader*. The book asks what a dashboard does, why it matters, what is risked when it is done poorly, what is gained when it is done well, how any of that is proved — and, running through all of it, how one might measure a vibe.

It is research about method. It never researches nightlife, never answers the prototype's questions about actual places, and never makes the creative decisions; those are Carly's.

## Where it stands

The book is written. Chapters 0 to 16 are at draft 2: each was framed in the research log before any research, researched by four parallel agents, spot-checked at source, read cold by an agent with no project context, re-traced by an independent verifier, and corrected. Carly has not yet read the chapters; nothing in them is finished until she has. Beside the chapters sit the things the last chapter pointed at: a rule register generated from every chapter's rules, a deviation log with its first entry, a library pass over the sources that could only be read as abstracts, and a list of probe candidates. `00-plan/STATE.md` has the detail and says what is open and whose it is.

## What to know before relying on it

- **Draft 2, unread by its author's client.** Carly sets the mission and is the final reader; her steer on any chapter has not yet been given.
- **Ten corrections are proposed and not applied.** The library pass (7 October) read in full thirty-one sources the chapters had read as abstracts or not at all, and most of those readings changed something. The ten that matter are listed at the head of `01-sources/LIBRARY-PASS.md` with the rule each bears on. The chapters still print the earlier wording, because the method is that an earlier chapter is not rewritten without Carly's word.
- **Where a chapter's rules table and the register differ on a tier, the register is current.** Chapter 16 re-based the tiers; the chapters' tables stand as printed. `06-register/rule-register.md` states each rule once, with every chapter row that states it.
- **Chapter 16 estimated about two hundred Fixed rules; the register has 280.** The chapter's postscript says what that changes.
- **Every claim says how it was read.** A dagger (†) in a chapter, or [abstract or secondary] in the reader, marks a figure or quotation not read in full; "(read once)" marks a quotation not re-checked. Fifty-four register rules rest on such a source and are flagged *full text not read*.
- **Chapters 6 to 12 were researched without web search** — by fetching known pages — and each names the canonical texts it could not reach. Chapters 13 to 16 had search rationed to thirty calls an agent.
- **Law is reported with its wording and date, never advised on.** Chapter 10 reads the data-protection statutes as they stood on 6 October 2026; Chapter 13 the Equality Act and the accessibility regulations.
- **Nothing has been tested with people.** The seventy-five probe candidates in `03-tests/PROBES.md` are candidates; none has been run. The nightlife illustrations in the chapters show method and decide nothing about any place.
- **Confidential material** — the Vibe Setting Framework and the 2026 data — is cited in the chapters and never reproduced.

## Regenerating

The chapter files are the record. After any chapter edit, from the repository root:

```
python3 05-book/build_reader.py --assets "<path to the nightlife-culture-design skill>/assets"
python3 06-register/build_register.py parse     # only if a chapter's rules table changed
python3 06-register/build_register.py build
```

The reader needs the Python packages `markdown` and `beautifulsoup4`. The register's `parse` step re-reads the compilation and Chapter 16's table; a new or reworded rule also needs a row in one of `06-register/work/classified-*.csv`.

## Rules that travel with the repository

The book never researches nightlife; that is Carly's knowledge and enters as the frame. It never answers the prototype's questions about actual places. It never makes the creative decisions. Confidential material is cited, never reproduced. Nothing earlier is rewritten on the spot: a finding that bears on an earlier chapter is logged and flagged, and the chapter changes on Carly's word. Agents never commit; Carly reviews and commits. Superseded files go to `archive/` or `_to_delete/`; nothing is deleted by an agent.
