A2. ACCESSIBILITY REGULATIONS — "DISPROPORTIONATE BURDEN"
====================================================================

URL: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/952/regulation/7
- "7.—(1) Regulation 6 does not require a public sector body to comply with the accessibility requirement if doing so would impose a disproportionate burden on the public sector body." (SP)
- "(2) A public sector body must perform an assessment of the extent to which compliance with the accessibility requirement imposes a disproportionate burden." (SV)
- "(3) In undertaking such an assessment, a public sector body must take account of relevant circumstances, including— (a) the size, resources and nature of the public sector body; and (b) the estimated costs and benefits for the public sector body in relation to the estimated benefits for persons with disabilities, taking into account the frequency and duration of use of the specific website or mobile application." (a and b SV)
- "(4) If, following the assessment, a public sector body determines that compliance with the accessibility requirement would impose a disproportionate burden, it must— (a) explain in its accessibility statement the parts of the accessibility requirement that could not be complied with; and (b) where appropriate, provide accessible alternatives to documents held by that public sector body that are not available on their website or mobile application." (a and b SV)
Regulation 8 (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/952/regulation/8, SP):
- "8.—(1) A public sector body must provide an accessibility statement in accordance with the model accessibility statement, and keep that statement under regular review."
- "(4) The accessibility statement must include— (a) an explanation of those parts of the content that are not accessible and the reasons why; (b) where appropriate, a description of any accessible alternatives provided; …"
Mechanism: the body itself decides after a mandatory assessment; the record is the published statement; nobody approves in advance; monitoring and enforcement come afterwards.

WHAT SURPRISED ME
=====================================================================
- The evidence that written reasons improve the decision is thin, and where measured directly it is null (Hoffmann) or negative (Wright's "space or random characters"). The supportable claim is narrower: reasons help when demanded before commitment, for a reader whose view is unknown.
- About half of overrides in Nanji were appropriate, ranging from 12% to 92% by rule type. A high override rate says as much about the rule as about the people overriding it.
- Ancker found no effect of workload or of time since deployment; what cut acceptance was the number of rules firing at once and repeats.
- Leape's phrase "the person responsible for documentation will ensure that all boxes are ticked", and that reported compliance in Ontario was 98%.
- Neither IETF, ISO nor W3C asks for a record when a SHOULD or a recommendation is not followed. The Code of Practice and the AQuA Book do. The record requirement comes from the statistical and analytical traditions, not the technical-standards one.
- Drawing on Courage is 2022 at the publisher, not 2021, and the Prototype sentence is a question.
