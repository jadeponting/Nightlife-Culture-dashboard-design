ITEM 1. ATMOSPHERE AS MADE

1a. Böhme, "Atmosphere as the Fundamental Concept of a New Aesthetics", Thesis Eleven 36 (1993). UNREACHABLE at source (SAGE page gives metadata only; open copies failed). No direct quotation. What I can give is SECONDARY, as quoted in Michels 2015 (open access), each [SV] as present in Michels:
- Böhme's term "aesthetic work" described as: "'making' atmospheres through work on an object" (Böhme 1993, p. 123, quoted by Michels, s.3.1).
- "the task of making [the] broad range of aesthetic reality transparent and articulable" (Böhme 1993, p. 125, quoted by Michels; square brackets are Michels's).
- Michels's gloss [SP]: "Böhme (1993, 2006) suggests that one thinks of atmospheres as "in between" subject and object, but also as prior to this very distinction."
- The phrase "the art of the stage set" is the title of Böhme's later paper (Ambiances, 2013), which I could not open. Do not attribute it to the 1993 paper without checking.
Type: critical/philosophical scholarship. Role: the "made" pole — atmospheres are produced by aesthetic work, so the makers are identifiable people.

1b. Michels, "Researching affective atmospheres", Geographica Helvetica 70, 255–263. Published 05 Oct 2015. Open access. https://gh.copernicus.org/articles/70/255/2015/ (PDF: .../gh-70-255-2015.pdf)

- "it is extremely difficult to be analytically precise when empirically researching atmospheres" [SV]
- The three methodological steps are his section 3 headings [SP for headings]: 3.1 Tracing the material modulation of atmospheres; 3.2 Exploring the sensual capacities of bodies; 3.3 Accounting for the (in)stability of atmospheres.
  Step 1 method: "taking on the role of a "sensory apprentice"" (citing Pink 2009) [SV], "by way of participant observation and (narrative) interviews" [SV].
  Step 2 method: "I present an auto-ethnographic field note" [SV].
  Step 3 method: "requires a researcher to "zoom out" and to describe similar compositions of affective atmospheres at other sites" [SV].
- His own caveat: "decomposing atmospheres along these lines may not resonate with other ways of conceptualising" [SV] (continues "...and researching affective atmospheres" [SP]).
- "Aesthetic work can be described as a process through which material components are selected and shaped" [SV].
Type: peer-reviewed methodological essay with one illustrative case (an artistic intervention in Berlin public space; read for method only). Strength: conceptual; single project; no validation. Role: the clearest statement of a qualitative three-step protocol and of its admitted imprecision.

- The chapter should cite the 2025 edition as current; I could not read either edition's text, so I cannot say what changed.
- Formulae (2019 edition, formulas A.1/A.2), SECONDARY via the Soundscapy tutorial by Mitchell, Aletta & Kang, https://soundscapy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/HowToAnalyseAndRepresentSoundscapes/ :
  ISOPleasant = [(pleasant - annoying) + cos45° * (calm - chaotic) + cos45° * (vibrant - monotonous)] * 1/(4 + √32)
  ISOEventful = [(eventful - uneventful) + cos45° * (chaotic - calm) + cos45° * (vibrant - monotonous)] * 1/(4 + √32)
  (the three bracketed terms and the scaling fraction each confirmed present on re-fetch; the page says the fraction scales coordinates to the range (-1, 1) [SP]).
- Medians: same page says it "takes inspiration from the recommendation in the ISO standard to use the median as a summary statistic" [SP]. I could not verify "interquartile range" or "range" against ISO text.

2d. Axelsson, Nilsson & Berglund, "A principal components model of soundscape perception", J Acoust Soc Am 128(5), 2836–2846, Nov 2010. ABSTRACT via Europe PMC DOI lookup (publisher page 403). Two fetches consistent; the second was a full verbatim print; no separate YES/NO pass because Europe PMC then rate-limited.
- "One hundred listeners measured 50 excerpts of binaural recordings of urban outdoor soundscapes on 116 attribute scales."
- "resulting in three components: Pleasantness, eventfulness, and familiarity, explaining 50, 18 and 6% of the total variance, respectively."
- "it suggests which basic dimensions are necessary to measure, how to measure them by a defined set of attribute scales, and how to promote high-quality soundscapes."
Strength: laboratory listening experiment, n=100, 50 recordings, one country (Sweden); PCA on averaged scale values. Note three components were found, not two; familiarity (6%) was dropped downstream. Role: the empirical origin of pleasant/eventful and hence of "vibrant".
- Independent confirmation of the sample in Lionello et al. 2021: "they were selected from the analysis of the principal components across 116 adjectives scaled by 100 students on 50 audio-recordings of urban locations in Sweden" [SV] — note "students".

2e. Aletta, Kang & Axelsson 2016, Landscape and Urban Planning 149. UNREACHABLE (abstract not obtained). SECONDARY definitions as quoted by Mitchell et al. 2020, each [SV as present there]: descriptors are "measures of how people perceive the acoustic environment"; indicators are "measures used to predict the value of a soundscape descriptor"; indices are "single value scales derived from either descriptors or indicators that allow for comparison across soundscapes". This descriptor / indicator / index distinction is directly reusable for the chapter.

2f. Mitchell, Oberman, Aletta, Erfanian, Kachlicka, Lionello, Kang, "The Soundscape Indices (SSID) Protocol", Applied Sciences 10(7), 2397, 1 April 2020. Open access. https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/10/7/2397

- "a minimum of 100 responses are collected at each location during multiple 2–5-h sessions" [SV]; no rationale for 100 is given (confirmed on re-fetch).

- "the questionnaire is primarily based on Method A of ISO/TS 12913-2:2018" [SV]; "5-point Likert scale" [SV], from 'Strongly Disagree (1)' to 'Strongly Agree (5)' [SP]; "the 8 scales which make up the circumplex model of the Swedish Soundscape Protocol" [SV].
