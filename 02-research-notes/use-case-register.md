# Use-case register — what the instrument is *for*, by user and moment

*`02-research-notes/`. From Carly's steer, 17 August 2026, structured for the chapter plan.
This register extends the brief; the chapter list is amended alongside it.*

The steer contains three distinct moves, each with named users and a display consequence.

---

## Move one — visibility is opportunity: the discovery surface

**The principle:** *"You cannot be what you cannot see."* If you don't know there's a buzzing
scene down the road, you stay socially excluded and miss the talent-incubation opportunities
nightlife culture delivers.

| User | Moment | What the display must do |
|---|---|---|
| **The kid who likes jungle** | Choosing a city — or a university | Find a place that *sounds like them*: discover place, find your tribe. "Bristol over Birmingham, because the scene" — through ticketing data |
| **The would-be participant nearby** | Any evening | Surface the scene down the road they didn't know existed |
| **Investors in the underground** — Arts Council England, PRS Foundation, philanthropists, brand sponsorship, capital funders, incubators | Deciding where to put money and attention | Measure and expose **opportunity for economic dynamism** — hone attention onto scenes ready to grow. "Leave something behind" beyond advocacy |
| **Journalists** | Looking for the story | The scene that's happening before it's famous |
| **Independent musicians and promoters** | Being found | The map as a place where their work is discoverable — shine the light on them |

**Where it lives:** **new Chapter 7 — Discovery: finding your tribe.** Inherits the identity
portraits (Ch 5, never ranked), the felt layer (Ch 4), and the map (Ch 3). Its research
questions: matching and recommendation display without ranking places; opportunity display
without triggering the extraction/speculation risks Ch 2 of the superseded work flagged
(the map "supplies the vocabulary and the coordinates"); how a scene consents to being
discoverable (the community register's data-ethics questions bind).

## Move two — the attribution flip: we attracted them here first

**The principle:** footfall and spend data (mobile, Visa) measure visits well but never the
**source** of the visit. Anonymised ticketing data can show that *the music event was the cause*
— the visit originated with a ticket. Then:

- **Economic impact:** the sector can say what its impact actually is — *"x% of overnight stays
  happened because we put an event on."*
- **Visitor levies:** when levies arrive, nightlife and festivals can claim a legitimate share
  of the benefit they create, instead of the arts-funded culture sector claiming the visit.
  *"We attracted them here in the first place — you got that metric BECAUSE of us."*
- **Destination management organisations** (Visit West, Visit Brighton): can legitimately bring
  nightlife into attraction strategies once origination is attributable.
- **Business Improvement Districts** *(added on steer, 17 Aug)*: once origination is
  displayable, the logic for supporting their levy payers stops being a retail story and starts
  being a hospitality-and-events story — a whole class of levy-funded local bodies whose case
  for backing the night this measure would make.
- **The mechanics are known, not speculative** *(steer)*: every ticket carries a postcode and a
  purchase timestamp; travel-time thresholds imply overnight stays; event end-time versus last
  transport implies taxi-or-bed; hotel capacity and price-spike data (held by destination
  organisations, and visible in the market — Manchester on match days, Brighton at Pride)
  corroborate. Promoters already know where their people come from. The work is **linking,
  anonymising and governing** existing data, not inventing a pipeline.
- **The reframe:** from "nightlife is a problem (transport, police safety data)" to **"WE are
  civic infrastructure — and the infrastructure of place should serve us, not limit us."**

**Where it lives:** **Chapter 6 — the measure on screen** takes attribution as a measure-design
question (what an origination measure is, what it commits to, how it displays without
overclaiming causality — the hostile-reader test bites hard on "because of us"). The flow
display of visits-from-source belongs to the flow-map family named in Ch 3 step zero. The civic
infrastructure framing is Ch 2's value column at policy scale.

## Move three — the dynamic census: measurement without the clipboard

**The principle:** the UK Music census is nearly unrepeatable because of sheer man-hours —
humans standing at doors on one night only. Ticketing-led measurement can be **dynamic, over
time** — economic impact as a living series, not a snapshot. This strengthens the studio's
value-created-versus-value-extracted ambition, and:

- **Standard economic development metrics are made by administrations and map what *they* care
  about** — social deprivation, job density, travel-to-work time measured on standard working
  hours. The night barely features. (The Greater London Authority did the night-time cut well;
  most places can't, or don't know why they should.)
- **The joy metric runs alongside them** — not replacing the existing methodologies, which
  already exist in numbers, but adding the layer they structurally omit.
- **The university story:** students move for the vibe; the university benefits; the city loses
  council tax; the night becomes "a problem to be managed." The instrument can tell the opposite
  story — the night *creating* the attractiveness — matched against population data: age,
  demographics, wage-to-rent, quality of life. **"Nightlife creates dynamism before it registers
  in the data."** Policymakers administrate the issues of successful density and take the labour
  for granted.

**Where it lives:** Chapter 6 (the measure: dynamic series versus snapshot; the leading-
indicator claim "dynamism before it registers" as a display of *time*), Chapter 8 (freshness —
the living series is the anti-observatory design), and the eventual prototype (this is the
strongest build-phase use case: the census that never needs a clipboard).

## Move four — the platform horizon *(added on steer, 17 Aug — hypothetical, post-prototype)*

**Not an early use case, and not for the prototype.** Recorded because it is the investible
model the earlier moves could grow into, and because one part of it — retention — is a design
requirement *now*.

**Partnership data spines.** Other organisations can also tell us what a place sounds like, and
discoverability benefits them too:

- **Beatport** — downloads and API; can inspire and route song purchases
- **Spotify logins** — taste-matching against the user's own listening, alongside artist
  performances from ticketing data (**Bandsintown already does this pairing** — the precedent
  exists)
- **Shazam searches** — where people reached for their phones to ask *what is this*
- **PRS music-recognition boxes** — venues submit playlists via recognition hardware; a
  partnership gives access to venue-side performance data *and* helps PRS get into more places.
  **Dual-benefit by construction** — the partnership shape the studio's principles require

**The affiliate return-path.** The easyJet pattern: the airline's margin is hotels and car
hire, not flights. The instrument's equivalent — discovery routes to tickets, song purchases,
merch — but **flipped to benefit music and musicians**: the affiliate margin flows to artists
and the fund, not away from them. A new model of affiliate marketing with the extraction
reversed — the value-created-versus-value-extracted thesis as a revenue mechanism.

**The retention requirement — this part binds the design now.** The dashboard survives by
repeat business: either a paid service whose insights are worth returning for, or **a
repeatable task** — and the candidate is event discovery, *beyond a basic listing site*. This
upgrades Chapter 7's status: discovery is not a later feature, it is the retention engine that
keeps the instrument alive between policy moments. (An advocacy tool is opened twice a year; a
discovery tool is opened twice a week — and the twice-a-week use is what keeps the data fresh,
the scenes visible and the instrument fundable.)

**Status:** hypothetical by Carly's own framing; nothing here enters a chapter as a commitment.
The design consequences that do bind: Ch 7 carries the repeatable-task requirement; Ch 6's
platform-dependency rule governs any partnership spine (owned or durably licensed, never at a
platform's pleasure); the return-path principle joins Fairbnb in Ch 7's revenue-return rule.

## Cross-cutting cautions carried from earlier chapters

- Discovery increases legibility, and Chapter 2 of the superseded research documented where that
  cuts: the map hands vocabulary and coordinates to place-marketing. The community register's
  question — *"how can the map actively redistribute opportunity, rather than simply display
  what already exists?"* — is the discovery chapter's governing test.
- The attribution claim will be attacked ("correlation, not source"). The measure chapter must
  design the claim's display to survive the hostile-reader test: what "originated with a ticket"
  can and cannot assert.
- A scene's discoverability is consented, per the data-ethics questions; "find your tribe"
  never becomes "find their scene before they wanted finding."
