## In one paragraph

Carly Heath (Nightlife Culture) asked for a research book on how dashboards work and why — the methodological foundation for the Vibe Map's evidence displays — written for someone meeting the subject for the first time, with every claim saying what kind of thing it is and how firm. Version 1 (fourteen chapters, August 2026) was archived on 5 October when she reset the brief (`00-plan/BRIEF-v2.md`). Version 2 was written between 5 and 7 October 2026: seventeen chapters, about 485,000 words, each researched by parallel agents, spot-checked at source, read cold, independently verified and corrected. The closing chapter assembled the book's own rules, records and tests; a register, a deviation log, a library pass and a list of probe candidates followed. Carly has committed all of it and has not yet read the chapters. Nothing is queued; what is open is hers to decide.

## What to know before relying on it

1. **It is draft 2 and unread by Carly.** Her steer on any chapter will change it.
2. **Ten claims are known to need correcting and have not been corrected.** `01-sources/LIBRARY-PASS.md`, first page. The heaviest: rule 7.2's ban on league tables and bands is not supported by the RSS working party it cites (the report permits both with uncertainty shown); Chapter 1's "clearest demonstration in any field" rests on nine of thirty-eight studies the authors call mostly confounded; Chapter 9's Fix My Street figure is 11.6 against 18.2 per cent from a study that says it "cannot estimate causal effects"; rule 11.11's "improved recall" is the opposite of what its source found; Chapter 16's truth 2 leans on a tier that could not be disobeyed. The chapters print the earlier wording because the method is that an earlier chapter is not rewritten without Carly's word.
3. **The register, not the chapters' tables, is the current statement of each rule's tier.** Chapter 16 re-based "Fixed" into three senses and retired "Fixed as a stance"; the chapters' tables stand as printed.
4. **Chapter 16's estimate of the Fixed count was wrong.** It expected about two hundred after re-basing; the register has 280 (74 per cent). The postscript at the end of Chapter 16 says what stands and what is reopened — chiefly whether 245 build checks is a list short enough to be obeyed.
5. **The marks matter.** † (in the reader, [abstract or secondary]) means not read in full; "(read once)" means not re-checked. Fifty-four register rules rest on such a source. Five daggers are stale — the ledger already records a full-text read (LIBRARY-PASS, first page).
6. **Chapters 6–12 were researched without web search**, by fetching known pages; each lists the canonical texts it could not reach. Twenty-one sources are open on the web but behind walls the fetch tool cannot pass; a person with a browser can read them (LIBRARY-PASS lists them).
7. **Law is reported, not advised.** Chapter 10's statutory map is as read on 6 October 2026; the book found the brief's own map out of date in four places.
8. **Nothing has been tested with people or with the prototype's data.** Every nightlife illustration is method only.
9. **`02-research-notes/ch16-assembly.md` cites Chapter 0 by the line numbers of its 6 October text**, which is kept at `archive/ch00-how-to-read-this-book-2026-10-06.md`.

## For an agent picking this up

- **To understand the research:** Chapter 0, then the reader's 60-second versions in order, then Chapter 16 and its postscript, then whichever chapter bears on the question in hand, with its entry in the research log beside it (the log says what the chapter expected to find, what it did find, and what the cold reader and the verifier made it change).
- **To check a claim:** find the rule in `06-register/rule-register.md`, follow its chapter rows to the chapter, the chapter's Sources section to the ledger, and the ledger to how the source was read. If the rule's status says *full text not read*, look for the source in LIBRARY-PASS.
- **You may:** read everything; answer questions about what the book found and how firmly; draft corrections, probes or protocols as proposals; regenerate the reader and the register.
- **You may not, without Carly's word:** change what a chapter says (log the finding and flag it instead); apply the ten corrections; decide anything in the open list; research nightlife or any real place; reproduce the confidential material; commit.
- **If you add work:** frame it in the research log before you start, as a new entry; put sources in the ledger with how each was read; update this file.

## The working method, in one breath

Frame the chapter's questions in the log before researching · parallel research agents, one facet each, briefed to fetch at source, quote exactly, follow threads and report the unasked-for · orchestrator verifies load-bearing claims at source, convenient findings first · run the symmetry test on the studio's own findings before writing · write to the teaching template in the studio's voice · append the log entry with the backward look and flags · ledger entries · cold-reader and verification passes (ask the verifier explicitly for paraphrases in quotation marks and for silent truncations) · deliver, place in repo, update this file · continue without waiting for approval. Full method in `AGENTS.md`.

## Standing rules

Agents never commit. Nothing is written outside this repository. Confidential material (Vibe Setting Framework; 2026 data) is cited, never reproduced. Nightlife is never researched; the prototype's questions are never answered; the creative decisions are Carly's. Nothing is deleted by an agent: superseded files go to `archive/` or `_to_delete/`.
