## What changed, in order of weight

1. **Zhi, Ottley & Metoyer 2019 (rule 11.11).** The why-cell says linking "improved recall with the slideshow layout". The paper says "We did not find any evidence for linking improving the recall task performance", and that linking made no difference to comprehension. What linking moved was self-reported engagement, time on the story and number of interactions. Analysed n is 167, on one story.
2. **Paterno et al. 2009 (rule 16.4).** "The severe rules are obeyed where set apart" rests on a tier that could not be disobeyed ("by design since this was a 'hard stop'"). The evidence that separation raises compliance is the middle tier only: 29 against 10 per cent. The lowest tier is not comparable.
3. **Tintarev & Masthoff 2012 (rule 12.26).** "Personalisation raised satisfaction" is the abstract's "may improve"; in the experiment with real trial of the items the satisfaction gain was absent and the plain baseline was the most effective, with the most opt-outs. The rule stands; the cell overstates one half.
4. **EHRC Code 2026, Chapter 7.** Now at source. The chapter never mentions websites; the capitalised "The duty is anticipatory" is not the Code's sentence.
5. **Stevens 1946.** Stevens's own sentence ties scale type to "the basic empirical operations performed"; the "questions we intend to ask" wording is not his. His "pragmatic sanction" for averaging ordinal scores is available to quote.
6. **Weissgerber et al. 2024.** Citation corrected (D.I.P. Terhorst; full title; pp. 293–318); it is a single replication and carries only the null, not "slower; less confident".
7. **Glaeser, Kim & Luca.** "We have little to say about causation"; the 29.2 per cent is about establishment counts and is "more accurate for denser, wealthier, and more educated ZIP codes".
8. **Javed et al. 2010.** Sixteen participants; speed, not correctness; time series, not profiles.
9. **EN 301 549 V4.1.1.** Clause 9 is aligned to WCAG 2.2 and adds 9.7, "User preferences for web pages" — new to the ledger.

Supported as stated, nothing to change: Zikmund-Fisher 2011 (check two sentence wordings if quoted); Lam 2008; Birt 2016; Böhme 1993; the aria-live table (1.0 edition); the Dublin Core frequency term.

## What surprised me

- **Zhi et al.'s printed means contradict the paper's own direction.** The score is "the task completion time divided by the proportion of correct responses" and "Higher scores indicates lower performance", yet the slideshow layout, reported as better, has the higher mean in both places (533 against 398 for comprehension; 139 against 97 for recall under linking). Either the labels are swapped in print or the direction is. Someone should look at Figures 5 and 6 in the PDF before the chapter leans on the layout finding at all.
- **A third of the "unreachable" list is not a library problem.** RFC 9110, DCAT 3, Media Queries 5, the aria-live table, EN 301 549 clause 9, the PsyArXiv preprint, the two HAL papers, and six PubMed Central papers (Dobres, Dong 2020, Dong 2022, van der Sijs, Spiegelhalter 2005b, probably Nanji) are all free to a person with a browser.
