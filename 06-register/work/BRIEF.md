# Classification brief — the rule register (from Chapter 16 and Chapter 0)

## Chapter 0's tier table (revised 7 October 2026)



The book uses the Decision Book's three tiers, and they answer "can this be crossed?" directly:

| Tier | Plain meaning | Breaking it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed** | **A hard line**, in one of three senses, which the register names for every Fixed rule. *As a requirement*: there is a right answer and it can be checked — contrast, keyboard access, disclosure thresholds, provenance on every number, no league table of identities, no implied counterfactual — and the build enforces it as a check that fails. *As a sequence*: the order of operations is the hard line (define the construct before the proxy; check recognition before release; write the retirement plan before launch) while the form each step takes is Adaptable, so the rule carries both tiers. *As a statement of the record*: a finding the book is confident of, from which a design rule may or may not follow — a truth, listed apart, and not a rule at all. | Negligence, for a requirement or a sequence. Do not do it for taste |
| **Adaptable** | **A default you may argue with.** There is no single right answer; the book states a position and the reasons. You may take a different one if you can defend it and you log it. Where the position is the house's own, held with its grounds, the register says *Adaptable (house default)*. | The job. Deviation is expected — with a record |
| **Decorative** | **Free.** Register, imagery, pacing, texture — provided nothing that carries meaning lives only there. An expressive element is Decorative only if it can be ignored; one that cannot — the W3C's "specific sensory experience" — is a tier of *element*, not of rule, and owes identification in text (Chapter 14). | Expected |

Two wordings you will meet in the chapters' tables that this legend no longer uses. Chapters 5 to 15 printed **Fixed as a stance** (or "(stance)") for a rule the studio adopts whose source is a reading or a practice, and **Fixed as a procedure** (or "(procedure)") for a rule that fixes the steps and not the parameter. Chapter 16 counted them — 90 and 102 rows — and re-based them: a stance is *Adaptable (house default)* unless the chapter's own grounds show a requirement, and a procedure is a requirement (the procedure is the check) or a sequence, by its wording. The rule register carries the re-based tier; the chapters' tables stand as printed, with the register as the current statement. Chapter 16 also reconciled the eleven rules that carried different tiers in different chapters, and the register marks the superseded rows. Where you deviate from a Fixed or Adaptable rule, Chapter 16 gives the nine-line deviation entry to write first and the test to run, and section 8 of that chapter the build's checks.



## Chapter 16 §3 — the senses and the re-basing

### What the count means

Three readings are possible and the chapter takes the third. The first is that dashboards are a subject of right answers, so the book's 89 per cent is the subject's; the Decision Book's 74 per cent, on the same kind of material, says otherwise. The second is that the chapters' "Fixed as a stance" is a third tier in disguise — the house's position, held with reasons, which the Decision Book would call Adaptable with a strong default — and that re-labelling 90 rows would bring the book to about 70 per cent, near the Decision Book; that is part of the answer. The third is that the book has been using Fixed to mean "important", which is the drift Chapter 0's gap row foresaw, and that the cost is the one section 6 measures in other fields: a rule set in which nearly everything carries the strongest word is a rule set in which the word is read as noise. The road-design manual's and the coding standard's top tiers are short; RFC 2119's own instruction is "sparingly"; the clinical evidence is that acceptance falls with every extra rule that fires. *(Reading.)*

So the register in section 8 does three things to the tiers. It gives Fixed one of three senses, each with its test: *Fixed as a requirement* — a right answer that can be checked, whose breach is negligence, which the build enforces as a check that fails (contrast; disclosure thresholds; the floor; expressiveness in Chapter 14's sense); *Fixed as a sequence* — an order of operations that is the hard line while each step's form is open (construct before proxy; recognition before release); and *Fixed as a statement of the record* — a finding the book is confident of, from which a design rule may or may not follow, and which is not a rule at all but a truth, listed separately. Everything the chapters called "Fixed as a stance" becomes *Adaptable (house default)* — the house's position, held with its grounds, which a later display may leave with a logged reason — unless the chapter's own grounds show it to be a requirement, in which case it is one. And "Fixed as a procedure" becomes either a requirement (the procedure is the check) or a sequence, by its wording. The result, estimated from the compilation and confirmed when the register is built, is a Fixed list of perhaps two hundred rules, most of them requirements the build can test, and an Adaptable list of the rest — around two hundred and fifty before the forty-two duplicate groups collapse — with their grounds. That is the shape the fields recommend and the shape the evidence says is read. *(Nightlife Culture; Reading.)*

The W3C's fourth category, which Chapter 14 found the tiers lack — the "specific sensory experience" that may not be ignored and need not carry data — is not a tier of rule but a tier of *element*, and belongs in the boundary log's "tier claimed" line, where Chapter 14 put it. The register does not add it as a fourth tier of rule; it notes, under the Decorative tier, that an expressive element is Decorative only if it can be ignored, and otherwise is a sensory experience owing identification. *(Reading.)*

## Chapter 16 §7 — the reconciliations

### The rule register: 486 rows, and what is wrong with them

The counts and the tier distribution are in section 3. Four more findings bear on the register's form.

*The same rule is stated twice, forty-two times.* The compilation groups 42 sets of rules stated in more than one chapter in different words — the precise twin (D1), the table route (D2), the sentence first (D3), the source line (D4), the dates (D5), the comparison gate (D6), ranks and intervals (D8), identity never ranked (D9), uncertainty as a range (D10), a cell not shown is marked (D11), nothing crucial behind a hover (D12), colour never the only carrier (D13), recognition before release (D14), construct before proxy (D15), the denominator (D16), the unit declared (D17), release the data (D18), defaults declared (D19), monitoring not evaluation (D20), the retirement plan (D22), revisions and status marks (D23), the disclosure floor (D25), outliers (D26), measuring use and trust (D28), the phone first (D34), "not done" written (D37), and the rest. Each group is one rule in the register with the chapters that state it listed as its sources, the strictest consistent wording kept, and the Decision Book's rule BRK-11 applied to the book's own text: stated once, pointed to from the rest. *(Reading; the compilation.)*

*The Kind column outgrew its legend.* Five kinds defined in Chapter 0, with Evidence of method added by the chapters; 182 distinct wordings printed; 141 of them used once; 163 of 486 rows (34 per cent) use one of the five words alone; thirty sub-kinds of Standard introduced chapter by chapter; fourteen rows with "—". Chapter 0's rule that a Standard "says whether it *binds* Nightlife Culture or is *adopted voluntarily*" is met in eight rows. The register keeps the five kinds, with Evidence of method as a sub-kind of Evidence, keeps the sub-kind of Standard as a second field since the chapters found it useful (regulator; law; method; design system; official statistics; W3C; government guidance; community), and adds a one-letter field for whether a Standard binds the studio or is adopted, which Chapter 0 asked for and nearly every row omits. *(Reading; the compilation.)*

*Seventeen Fixed rules are sourced only to the book.* Five rows cite the chapter itself and nothing else, three a section of the same chapter, nine other chapters of this book or version 1; all seventeen are Fixed in some form; four of them are the rules that carry a record (the claim record, the comparison record, the recognition record, the precedent record). A rule sourced only to the house is the house's position, and under section 3's re-basing it is *Adaptable (house default)* unless its grounds are a requirement; the four record-carrying rules are sequences — the record is written before the thing ships — and are tiered so. *(Reading; the compilation.)*

*Forty-nine Fixed rows rest on a dagger and none carries the flag.* Chapter 0's rule is that "A Fixed rule resting on a † source is flagged `[Further research]` until the full text has been read." Forty-nine Fixed-bearing rows have a dagger in their Source or Kind cell; no row carries the flag; Chapters 1 to 7 carry it for the whole table in the table's note, Chapters 8 to 11 do not, and Chapters 12 and 14 have dagger rows and no note. The register gives every such row the flag as a status, and the build lists them. *(Reading; the compilation.)*

### The rules that pull against each other, reconciled

*Ranking.* Chapters 1 and 2 allow a ranking with intervals; Chapters 7, 10 and 12 forbid a league table of places; Chapters 12 and 15 regulate the ordering the three forbid. The register resolves it as three rules at three tiers. *Fixed as a requirement*: no screen, export or crop carries an ordered all-places list of places on any measure (Chapters 7, 10, 12; version 1's crop check). *Fixed as a requirement*: any ordering of anything the dashboard does show — a ranking by rate as a screening instrument, a position within a distribution — carries its interval and the official wording (Chapters 1, 2, 12). *Adaptable (house default)*: whether the dashboard shows position at all, and in what form (Chapter 7's categories of statistical separation). Chapter 15's usage text (15.12) is the requirement's text. The three chapters that forbid and the two that regulate were never in conflict about a league table; they were in conflict about whether a screening ranking with intervals is one, and Chapter 7's own gap row records that its first draft "would have forbidden the chapter's own recommended forms". The register says: it is not, provided the interval and wording travel, and the crop check proves they do. *(Reading.)*

*Uncertainty.* "A numeric range, never a verbal hedge" (Chapters 1, 2, 15) and "Never a bare interval" (Chapter 3) are reconciled as Chapter 3 intended and no table said: the range is present, in numbers and in words (requirement); the *drawing* of it is not a bare error bar where a better form is available (Chapter 3's Adaptable list: countable dots, gradients, violins, draws), and the drawing's form is Adaptable (house default) with Chapter 3's grounds. *(Reading.)*

*Measuring use.* Chapters 1, 10 and 11 have the dashboard count its own use; Chapter 15 says reach is measured "only by asking". Both stand, as two rules: the site counts *how* it is used, never *who*, in aggregate and dated (Chapters 10, 11; requirement), and that count is never reported as *reach* or as *use by readers*, which is measured only by asking, with the response rate printed (Chapter 15; requirement). The conflict was in the word "use", which the first three used for events and the fourth for people. *(Reading.)*

*One tap or one step.* Chapters 4 to 7 say "one tap", Chapters 11, 13 and 14 "one step", and Chapter 11 asked whether the legend should carry "on the face / one step away / on request". The register adopts Chapter 11's three positions as its vocabulary — *on the face*, *one step away*, *on request* — maps "one tap" to "one step away", and lists Chapter 11's constraint as the requirement: nothing the majority of readers need is one step away. Chapter 0's glossary takes the three. *(Reading.)*

*Text inside the chart frame.* Chapter 12's rule that every chart carries its title, unit, source, base, address and date inside its frame departs from official practice and from the design system's preference for text outside images, and Chapters 12 and 13 made the departure "in addition to page text, never instead". The register keeps it as *Adaptable (house default)* with the condition Chapter 13 stated for the accessibility criterion, and the departure is this chapter's own first deviation entry in section 11's example, because it is a house rule that departs from a cited standard with a reason and a test (the crop test) and is exactly what the log is for. *(Reading.)*



## Chapter 16 §7 — the tests by tier sense

### The tests: fifty-five, by form, and what the break test actually is

Fifteen sections, nineteen paragraphs, 55 separate tests named: reader test 27, removal 10, record check 7, recomputation 6, adversarial walk 4, and one battery that fits no single form (Chapter 13's scan, two walks and two readers). By the leading test of each chapter: reader test in eleven, removal in two (Chapters 8 and 14), adversarial walk in one half (Chapter 10), record check in one (Chapter 15), recomputation in none. The chapters from 1 to 7 end their test by sending the break to "the four-line record Chapter 16 describes"; Chapters 8 to 11 do not say to log anything; and from Chapter 7 the section's job changes, from testing a break of a Fixed rule to testing any departure, and in Chapters 12 and 13 it is the page's release test whether or not anything is broken. Version 1's closing chapter said "almost every test turns out to be a form of removal or of adversarial walking"; version 2's tests, counted, are mostly a form of asking the reader the rule protects what they took from the display, which is what Chapter 1 said and twenty-six later tests repeated. The Decision Book's BRK-07, "Run the removal test for the relevant chapter", names the test two chapters lead with. *(Reading; the compilation.)*

The register resolves this by tying the form of the test to the sense of the tier, which is what the chapters were doing without saying so. A *requirement* is proved by the check that can fail: a recomputation (the chain arithmetic; the composite from its parts), a record check (the clause quoted; the nine lines filled), an automated scan, a measured removal (the crop test's pass rate; the four removals). A *sequence* is proved by a record check of dates and order (the record's first line dated before the thing it governs). An *Adaptable* rule's departure is proved by a reader test — the reader the rule protects, asked what the display says, compared with what was meant — or an adversarial walk where the rule protects against a hostile reader. So the build runs the requirement checks and the sequence checks, and the deviation log's test line names the reader test or walk for the rest, with its result. Version 1's three build checks and the fourth this chapter adds are in section 8. *(Reading; Nightlife Culture.)*



## Chapter 16 §8 — the register's fields and first rows

## 8 · The build's register

### The file

The register is one file in the repository, generated from the chapters' tables as the Decision Book's is generated from its chapters ("Generated from `chapters/` by `build/build_register.py`. Do not edit by hand — edit the chapter."), and the compilation made for this chapter is its first input: every one of the 486 rows with its file line. The build keeps the compilation's row identifiers (chapter.row) as the trace back to the chapter and gives each *register* rule — one per group after de-duplication — an identifier of its own, provisionally `DD-nnn`, since the house's prefix scheme is the house's. Each register row carries ten fields; all but *cited by* are decided by this chapter from what the chapters printed.

*Rule*: one wording, the strictest the source chapters share, with the chapters' variants kept in the compilation and not in the register. *Tier*: one of five — *Fixed (requirement)*, *Fixed (sequence)*, *Adaptable (house default)*, *Adaptable*, *Decorative* — with a second tier column for the open part of a rule whose order is fixed and whose form is not. *Kind*: one of Chapter 0's five, with the sub-kind of Standard in a second field and a one-letter field for whether a Standard binds the studio or is adopted. *Source*: the chapters' sources, and the chapters themselves by row. *Chapter*: the chapters that state the rule, first the one that made it. *Test*: the form — recomputation, record check, scan, removal, reader test, adversarial walk — and, for a requirement, the check the build runs; for a sequence, the records and dates compared; for an Adaptable rule, the reader the rule protects and the question. *Status*: current; superseded by; disputed; awaiting Carly; and the dagger status, *full text not read*, which the build lists. *Cited by*: the chapters and truths that lean on the rule, filled by the build and not by this chapter. *Record*: which section of the one record the rule writes to, where it writes to one. *Statement of the record*: for the rows that are truths and not rules, which Chapter 0's third sense names and the tables used for none — listed apart, under the chapters' "What holds true regardless", with the register pointing at them.

The estimated shape after re-basing, from the compilation and to be confirmed when the file is built: of 433 Fixed rows, the 212 unmarked and the 102 procedures sort into requirements and sequences by their wording, and most are requirements the build can test (contrast; thresholds; the floor; the furniture on the face; the lines of a record; the dates); the 90 stances become Adaptable (house default) except where their grounds show a requirement; the 45 Adaptable and 8 Decorative rows stand. The 42 duplicate groups collapse into one rule each. A Fixed list of roughly two hundred, most of them checks; an Adaptable list of roughly two hundred and fifty, each with its grounds. The Decision Book's 74 per cent Fixed, on this count, is the higher of the two. *(Nightlife Culture; the compilation.)*

### The checks

Version 1 specified three build-time checks "in the Decision Book's enforcement spirit (a rule that can be measured is a rule that is run)" and seeded no register to run them against. This chapter re-bases the three on version 2's rules and adds a fourth, and separates checks that fail the build from reports that list.

*The crop check* (version 1): "no exportable state renders an ordered all-places list". Re-based to the three ranking rules of section 7: every exportable state, screenshot region and share is tested for an ordered list of places on any measure, and for the interval and official wording travelling with any ordering that is shown; Chapter 12's measured crop test — a screenshot of each panel put before someone who has not seen the page, scored for what it is about, unit, source, date, gate and the other column — is the reader half, run per release, and its pass rate is written in the record. Fails the build on the first half; reports the second.

*The recipe check* (version 1): "every displayed composite recomputes from its displayed parts". Re-based to Chapter 5's rules and Chapter 8's chain arithmetic: every composed number is recomputed from the parts shown on its face and one step away, and every live element's arithmetic is rerun with the current data; the recipe section of the record is where the parts are declared. Fails the build.

*The state-honesty check* (version 1): "every reachable state carries heartbeat, gaps-with-reasons, and threshold behaviour". Re-based to Chapter 8's freshness statement (the data's date, the refresh, the last good copy with its age in the degraded state), Chapter 4's kinds of nothing (every empty cell marked with its kind, never blank), and Chapter 10's floors (every view's disclosure floor set before the view ships, and a room under both floors shown as present and unmeasured, never absent). Walks every reachable state. Fails the build.

*The removal check* (this chapter, from Chapter 14): every expressive element has an entry in the deviation log of the expressive type, with its four removals and the fifth check run and their results written, and its precise twin one step away. Fails the build where the entry is missing; reports where a removal failed, because the decision to ship a failed removal is a logged departure, not a build error.

And two reports, not gates: the *stale-record report* — every section of the record whose review date has passed, and every entry of the deviation log whose review date or lapse condition has passed, listed at each build, after the accessibility statement's "last reviewed" line, the datasheet's maintenance questions and the monitoring body's finding that many statements had not been reviewed in a year; and the *register report* — the rules whose status is disputed, awaiting Carly or full-text-not-read, and the record's lines that say "not done", counted, since the one measurement of such documents found the limits section filled one time in six. The sunset principle behind both is older than any of this: in one jurisdiction legislative instruments are "automatically repealed on set dates … 10 years after the instrument is registered", because "Sunsetting ensures that legislative instruments are kept up-to-date and only remain in force for as long as they are needed." (read once; fragments verified). A register entry nobody has re-read by its date is the shuttle's waiver, not a rule. *(Nightlife Culture; Reading; Standard (government guidance).)*

### The gate on the log

The one check that makes the deviation log a record: no change to the build that departs from a rule tiered Fixed (requirement) or Fixed (sequence) is merged without a deviation entry of the departure type in the same change, with its status *proposed* and its checker named; the build fails on a departure without an entry, and the entry's status moves to *accepted* only by the named checker. This is Lethbridge's one condition under which records are kept — "attached to the process for completing a change request" — and the government standard's "stored in version control so there is a record of what was changed, who by, and when". With the entry present, the requirement check the departure breaks is marked as a logged exception by the entry's identifier, so the check still runs on everything else and the exception is visible in the register report. For Adaptable rules, the entry is written when the departure is made and the build does not gate it; the stale-record report lists entries without a review date. *(Reading; Nightlife Culture.)*
