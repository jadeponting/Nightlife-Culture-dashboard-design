## How to read the evidence column

Every quotation below was returned by WebFetch, which reads the page through a small model. Two marks say how far each can be trusted.

- **STRING-VERIFIED** — the page was fetched a second time and asked YES or NO for the exact string, in a list that also held one invented control string; the string came back YES and the control came back NO. This is a model-level presence check, not a byte comparison.
- **SINGLE PASS** — returned once, not re-checked. Treat as a pointer to the passage, not as a quotation fit to print.

"Page" numbers are the reader's report of the PDF and are all SINGLE PASS.

## What the readings change, in order of weight

1. **RSS 2005 against row 7.2.** The working party permits a league table with ranking uncertainty shown and "considerable caution", and permits banding with an allowance for uncertainty. Row 7.2 forbids both and cites the report in support. The report backs Chapter 16's second ranking rule (any ordering carries its interval) and not the first.
2. **Kaliisa against row 1.7.** The proxy-moves-first finding is nine studies, five to four, and the authors call the larger effects confounded or uncontrolled. "The clearest demonstration in any field" is more than the review claims. A quotation slip sits in the same paragraph.
3. **Sjoberg against row 9.17.** 399,364 is reports from 154,957 people; the measured thing is a problem marked fixed by someone else, unverified; 11.6 per cent against 18.2 per cent; "we cannot estimate causal effects per se". The study is about being answered by action, not about being told.
4. **Birt against rows 2.23 and 9.33.** In the authors' own synthesised member check the return to the data "proved unnecessary". The paper is a method and a critique, not a demonstration that the dialogue changes the account.
5. **Mogstad against row 7.2.** The rank sets for the 50 largest commuting zones are "small". The paper marks where ranking survives as well as where it fails.
6. **Becker against row 5.5's reason.** The gap between weight and importance cannot always be closed by re-weighting; with correlated parts it needs negative weights.
7. **Yang against row 3.14.** "these 5 studies", not "all 5 studies"; and the warning reduced the effect without removing it.
8. **Berg & Pattillo and Kaminsky against row 6.11.** The open copies are the working papers. Kaminsky's record is in-sample. Berg and Pattillo's working paper reads "mixed but somewhat encouraging"; the sentences the chapter quotes belong to the journal abstract only.
9. Minor caveats: van der Bles (United Kingdom samples; contestedness not manipulated), Lorenz (factual tasks; error not worsened), Kossinets (one network; "may not be generalizable"), Haklay (the proportions vary by orders of magnitude), Hájek (the practical problem of which class to use "remains").
