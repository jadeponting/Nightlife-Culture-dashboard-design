# Working record · Chapter 6 — Finding value in the data

*How this chapter was made. These sections stood in the chapter at draft 2 and were moved here, word for word, on 14 October 2026 when the book was finished. They are the record of the method: what the chapter expected against what it found, what it did to the chapters before it, the gaps it met, where it met the design system and the Decision Book, and the loose ends it flagged. Nothing here is a rule; the rules are in the register, the open choices in the decision log, the probes in `03-tests/PROBES.md`.*

## Before you start

This is the sixth chapter of a research book written for Nightlife Culture, a creative policy studio led by Carly Heath, about how dashboards work and why. The book exists to support the Vibe Map — a planned public, non-commercial, interactive account of the people, activity, value and support systems that sustain a place's nightlife culture, built first for the West of England. You do not need to have read Chapters 1 to 5 to read this one. Four things from them recur: the working definition — *a dashboard is a display that turns data into a decision for a named reader*; Chapter 4's rule that a number is undefined until its unit, period and base are stated, and its warning that a platform's data describes the platform's users; Chapter 5's rule that a measure begins with a sentence saying what it is a measure of, and its finding that a measure once used changes what it measures; and the brief's own phrases for what this chapter is after — *how is value created here*, *value created and value extracted*, and *the hollow success*: a place where every standard marker is met and nobody local goes. Where this chapter refers to Carly, it means the studio's founder; where it refers to the Vibe Map, it is illustrating, not specifying. The studio's rulebook for design decisions is the Decision Book; its visual rules are in a design system (Style Guide v1.5).

Two conventions, explained fully in Chapter 0. Every rule carries a **kind** — *Standard*, *Evidence*, *Reading*, *Practice* or *Nightlife Culture* — and a **tier**: *Fixed*, *Adaptable* or *Decorative*; "Fixed as a stance" marks a rule the studio adopts whose source is a reading or a practice, and "Fixed as a procedure" fixes the steps and not the parameter. Flags in square brackets mark loose ends: `[Further research]`, `[Prototype flag]`, `[Validate with users]`, `[Revisit → Ch n]`, `[Revisit at end]`, `[Rich reference]` and `[Counterbalance → Ch n]`. A dagger (†) marks a source read at the authors' abstract only; "secondary" marks a quotation reached through another named document; "reconstructed" marks an abstract rebuilt from an index, which is weaker still.

A note on this chapter's sources. The research agents who gathered them had no web search for this sprint, only direct fetching, and the scholarly web is largely closed to a fetch tool — so several canonical texts (Tukey on exploratory analysis; Tversky and Kahneman on the law of small numbers; Holling on resilience; Hotelling's letter that began the travel-cost method; the Warwick Commission's figure for who the arts audience is) were not reached and are not quoted. Where that happens the chapter says so and uses what was reached. Every quotation that is given was confirmed present on its page by a second fetch, spot-checked by the author, or re-traced by an independent verifier; the few that are weaker than that are marked where they occur (†, "reconstructed", "secondary"). Some terms from earlier chapters recur without re-argument: the *symmetry test* (Chapter 5: any standard the chapter applies to others' instruments it applies to the studio's own, before writing); the *failure register* (the brief's list, in Carly's words, of the ways a dashboard fails — among them "if a viewer reads it and asks 'what am I looking at'" and if people "do not recognise their place within the front-end interface"); the *card* (Chapter 5's recipe card beneath any composed number); the *price list* (Chapter 5: the rate at which an index trades one part for another); the *one-tailed question* (Chapter 5: whether an instrument is built to find shortfall, thriving, or both); and the *three user groups* (the studio's named readers for testing). Four acronyms appear in quotations from official statistics: MSOA (middle-layer super output area, a census geography of 2,000–6,000 households), FRS (the Family Resources Survey), IDBR (the Inter-Departmental Business Register) and ACS (the US American Community Survey).

## Looking back

Against Chapters 0 to 5, the archived version 1, and the studio's Decision Book.

- **Strengthens** Chapter 1's truth that a published measure changes what it measures, with the strongest case yet — Scott's forest, which grew to resemble its own measurement — and **counterbalances** it: here the measure changed the thing itself rather than the behaviour around it, nobody gamed anything, and the failure took a generation to appear. The hollow success is reactivity plus a lag. `[Counterbalance → Ch 1]`
- **Strengthens** Chapter 2's finding that the official reader needs the official method, and **opens a thread**: the official method moved in 2026. The Green Book that Chapter 2 cited is withdrawn; its successor replaces the quintile table with a formula and requires weighted beside unweighted. Chapter 2's "contributed to" — the Decision Book's own tone example — is now the verb the Magenta Book licenses from theory-based evaluation.
- **Strengthens** Chapter 3's time-series rules with the control chart, whose three lines the data sets, and its small-multiples rule with the seasonal baseline (this month against this month in past years); **strengthens** its uncertainty rule with the ONS's warning that a modelled figure's interval cannot carry a trend arrow.
- **Strengthens** Chapter 4 at four points: the platform caveat now has the national statistics office's own footfall as its instance; "not yet" is now censoring, with a method; the outlier-as-question has a procedure and a false-alarm rate; and the provenance set now has a parameter-level cousin, the borrowed deadweight percentage. **Counterbalances** Chapter 4's outlier rule at small n, where the first explanation is sample size. **Opens a thread** on the reference period, which for the modelled small-area figure may not support a comparison at all.
- **Strengthens** Chapter 5 in three places: construct underrepresentation now has a clinical vocabulary and a grading (validated, reasonably likely, neither) for the card; the one-tailed question now has Wainer's arithmetic behind it; the symmetry test was run here on the studio's own claim ladder and ledger, and both survived amended. **Counterbalances** Chapter 5's recognition rule: shrinkage pulls the small place's figure toward its neighbours' regardless of what its people recognise, and the two can disagree. `[Counterbalance → Ch 5]`
- **Strengthens** version 1's Chapter 6 in its shape and **weakens** it in its labels. The claim ladder, the ledger strip, the counter-flows and the two-track display all survive as forms; the ladder's top line was a rung-one fact labelled as a rung-three claim, its lower rungs were euphemised as "associational", it lacked a deadweight line, and the "lead" in the two-track display was asserted where the literature requires it to be measured against a named reference series. Version 1's "what the night didn't originate" counter-flow is Easton's three readings in display form and is confirmed.
- **Opens a thread** for Chapter 0's legend: "reconstructed" (an abstract rebuilt from an index) is now used and should be defined; "Fixed as a procedure" is used more here than anywhere, for rules whose steps are fixed and whose parameter — run-length, base size, window, error rate — is open; and the chapter proposes that a "canonical truth" be defined by two tests, out-of-sample and the agreement of the competent, which Chapter 0 should either adopt or decline. `[Revisit at end]`
- **The Decision Book.** Chapters 5, 6, 8, 10 and 15 were read for rules on causation, disclosure, uncertainty and small numbers. **Agrees:** Chapter 5's tone example ("We cannot yet say how much of that fall the drop-in caused … What we can say is that young people used it, returned to it") is a contribution sentence and is this chapter's rung-one verb in practice; Chapter 10's "every arrow is a claim about order or causation" is Pearl's prohibition applied to diagrams; Chapter 8's and Chapter 15's disclosure rules (2/3/5 thresholds, secondary suppression, enforcement at query time, "suppressed to protect confidentiality" as a distinct message from "no data") are the confidentiality half of this chapter's two reasons; Chapter 15's "below a base of 30 the point estimate is replaced by a range; below the disclosure threshold it is suppressed entirely" is the two reasons operated separately, without naming them as two; Chapter 6's interval rule for modelled values agrees with the shrinkage display. **Conflicts:** none found, with one tension — Chapter 15's filter rule says a misleading combination "should suppress it or annotate it", and this chapter adds that every combination is a fork and the surprise of any one is judged against all of them, which is a rule about the whole filter set and not about any one combination. **Silent on:** gross against net and the deductions; distributional weights; the rung of a causal claim and the verb that matches it; leading indicators and measured leads; the exploratory status of every view, multiple comparisons and error control for highlighting; models' known wrongness and validation dates; control charts, common and special cause, and alert policy with its two numbers; seasonal baselines; shrinkage, modelled small-area figures and the no-arrow rule; the precision reason for withholding as distinct from confidentiality; and the identifying threshold set by a scene's knowability. The rules table above is the candidate for a Decision Book chapter on claims about value. `[Counterbalance → Decision Book]`

## Design system (Style Guide v1.5) and Decision Book — conflict log

**Agrees.** "Every big number carries a source line. A statistic without one is a claim, not a finding" extends here to a claim record: a value figure without its gross-or-net line, its rung and its small-number treatment is a claim. "Strong design must never make weak evidence look stronger than it is" is, in this chapter, the trend arrow on a modelled figure, the "significant" badge on a forked comparison, the alert without its false-alarm rate, and the rung-three verb on rung-one evidence. "Every comparison between places declares whether the comparability gate was cleared" now has a small-numbers clause: places of different sizes cannot be compared by raw rate without the interval. The Decision Book's tone example in Chapter 5 — the drop-in that cannot yet be said to have caused the fall — is this chapter's contribution sentence, already in the house voice; its Chapter 10 rule that every arrow is a claim about causation is Pearl's prohibition; its disclosure rules in Chapters 8 and 15 are the confidentiality half of the two reasons, and its Chapter 15 practice (a range below a base of 30, suppression below the disclosure threshold) operates the two reasons without naming them.

**Conflicts.** None direct. Two tensions. The design system's status chips mark a claim's firmness and the Decision Book's tiers mark a rule's; neither has a component for the *kind* of claim — observed, gross, net, contributed, caused, modelled, borrowed — which is what the claim record carries, and which a reader needs before firmness means anything. And the Decision Book's Chapter 15 treats a misleading filter combination one at a time ("suppress it or annotate it"); this chapter's garden treats the filter set as a whole, so that the rule about any one view depends on how many views there are. `[Counterbalance → design system; Decision Book Ch 15]`

**Silent on.** Sections 7 to 11 almost entirely: the gross-to-net arithmetic; rent against product; distributional weights; the rungs and the verb; leading indicators; the exploratory status of a view, multiple comparisons and error control; models' known wrongness and validation dates; control charts, common and special cause, alert policy and seasonal baselines; shrinkage and modelled small-area figures; the precision reason for withholding; the scene's knowability as the identifying threshold. The Decision Book's data chapters govern the chart and the table and stop before the claim; this chapter's rules table is the candidate for the chapter between them.
