# Working record · Chapter 16 — Breaking these rules well

*How this chapter was made. These sections stood in the chapter at draft 2 and were moved here, word for word, on 14 October 2026 when the book was finished. They are the record of the method: what the chapter expected against what it found, what it did to the chapters before it, the gaps it met, where it met the design system and the Decision Book, and the loose ends it flagged. Nothing here is a rule; the rules are in the register, the open choices in the decision log, the probes in `03-tests/PROBES.md`.*

## Before you start

This is the sixteenth and closing teaching chapter of a research book written for Nightlife Culture, a creative policy studio led by Carly Heath, about how dashboards work and why. The book exists to support the Vibe Map — a planned public, non-commercial, interactive account of the people, activity, value and support systems that sustain a place's nightlife culture, built first for the West of England. This chapter is different from the fifteen before it in one way: most of what it reads is the book itself. It assembles what the earlier chapters left — their rules, their records, their tests, their open questions and their quarrels with the house's design documents — and asks how a rule in this book is broken well. You do not need to have read the earlier chapters to follow the argument, because the chapter quotes what it needs and counts what it assembles; you will need them to use the register it leaves.

The chapter's conventions are the book's. Every finding is marked by **kind** — *Standard* (a published rule from a standards body, a regulator, a design system or a method text, with the sub-kind named: Standard (regulator), Standard (law), Standard (method), Standard (design system), Standard (official statistics)), *Evidence* (a measured result, with what was measured and on whom), *Evidence of method* (a published procedure whose results are reported — a sub-kind the chapters added to Chapter 0's five), *Reading* (this book's interpretation of evidence or standards), *Practice* (what a named body or project does, read for method), or *Nightlife Culture* (the house's own position) — and by **tier**: *Fixed* (holds in any dashboard), *Adaptable* (holds unless a stated reason overrides it) or *Decorative* (open). This chapter is where the tiers themselves are examined, so section 3 says what each has meant across the book and what it will mean in the register. A dagger (†) marks a figure or quotation taken from an abstract or a secondary account; "secondary" marks a source read only through another; "(read once)" marks a quotation returned once by the fetch tool without a presence check; "two passes" marks one returned identically twice without a presence check, and "three passes" three times. A presence check, or presence re-fetch, opens the page a second time and asks only whether the exact string is on it, with a false string beside it to show the tool will say no. Findings in the body carry their kind in italics at the end of a paragraph; tiers are given in the rules table. Flags in square brackets — `[Further research]`, `[Prototype flag]`, `[Validate with users]`, `[Revisit → Ch n]`, `[Revisit at end]`, `[Rich reference]`, `[Counterbalance → Ch n]` — are defined in Chapter 0; this chapter is where `[Revisit at end]` is answered.

The chapter's working words. The *Decision Book* is the house's design decision system (fifteen reference chapters and a rule register of 347 rows), and the *Style Guide* (v1.5) is the house's brand and design system; their rules are quoted with their file and line where it matters. The *tier test* is the Decision Book's first move on any disagreement: decide whether a rule is Fixed, Adaptable or Decorative before arguing about it. *Deference* is the Decision Book's name for treating an Adaptable decision as if it were Fixed; *negligence* for treating a Fixed one as if it were Adaptable. A *deviation* is a departure from a rule; the Decision Book names four kinds — "ignorant, lazy, expedient or earned" — in one checklist line and nowhere defines them, and section 4 does. The *override log* is the Decision Book's four-line record of a deliberate departure — "Trigger · rule and tier · alternative chosen · evidence"; the *boundary log* (Chapter 14) and the *precedent record* (Chapter 15) are this book's two further logs, and section 7 asks whether the three are one. The *rule register* is the list this chapter leaves: every rule in Chapters 1 to 16, with its tier, its kind, its source and its test. The *records beneath the dashboard* are the twelve documents Chapters 4 to 15 left, one each, which section 7 lists by name and line count. A *removal test* takes something away from a display and checks what remains; a *reader test* puts the display in front of a named kind of reader and compares what they take from it with what was meant; an *adversarial walk* goes to a state of the display with bad intent; a *recomputation* rebuilds a number from its parts; a *record check* inspects a document. These, with the *automated scan* that checks a built page by machine (Chapter 13), are the six forms the book's break tests take, counted in section 7. *Comply or explain* is the corporate-governance regime in which a company may depart from a code if it explains; *disproportionate burden* is the accessibility regulations' name for a self-assessed exemption; a *waiver* is a regulator's permission to depart from a rule; a *concession* and a *deviation permit* are the quality-management vocabulary for permission after and before the fact; *exception reporting* is the family-doctor contract's mechanism for excluding a patient from a target. An *architecture decision record* is software's one-file-per-decision convention; a *preregistration deviation* is a departure from a study's registered plan; a *changelog* is a dated list of a product's notable changes; a *datasheet* and a *model card* are machine learning's one-document conventions. *Alert fatigue* is the clinical finding that rules which fire too often are overridden by reflex. *Version 1* is this book's archived first draft; its Chapter 14 is this chapter's predecessor and is tested in section 10. The *build* is the prototype's engineering, which inherits the register; the *reader* is the book's compiled edition; the *ledger* is the sources file, `SOURCES.md`. From earlier chapters: the *precise twin* is the plain version of an expressive display, one step away (Chapter 14); the *four removals* — mono, silent, still, undressed — and the *fifth check* are Chapter 14's test; the *gate* is the declared basis on which two things may be compared (Chapter 7); the *kinds of nothing* are Chapter 4's names for an empty cell; the *held layer* is data shown coarser, or not at all, by the scene's decision (Chapter 10); the *floor* is a disclosure or precision threshold (Chapters 9 and 10); the *furniture* is what sits on a panel's face beside its figure (Chapter 11); the *crop test* is Chapter 12's measured test of a panel cut from its page; the *eight graders* are Chapter 2's readers; *Chartability* is the accessibility audit workbook Chapter 13 adopted; *ELS* is the ONS's Explore Local Statistics service (Chapter 15); a *DPIA* is a data protection impact assessment (Chapter 10); *TQV* is the Code of Practice's Trustworthiness, Quality and Value.

## 10 · The symmetry test

The brief's words for the chapter were "Tiers, deviation, the override log, the assembled removal tests, the gap register, the rule register for the build." Version 1's closing chapter answered them in 1,325 words by transfer. This chapter tested each, and the test of the chapter is what the testing found against what version 1 said.

*"Circa 150 Fixed-tier rules across Chapters 1–13."* Version 2 has 486 rules across fifteen chapters and 433 carry Fixed; version 1's estimate was a third of the count and the register it seeded was never written. The chapter's finding is that the count is the problem, not the seed. *Weakened, and replaced.*

*"Almost every test turns out to be a form of removal or of adversarial walking."* Counted, 27 of 55 are reader tests, 10 removals, 4 adversarial walks; version 1's table assembled thirteen tests under a pattern they did not have. The pattern version 2's tests do have is tied to the tier's sense in section 7. *Weakened, and replaced.*

*"The four kinds of deviation … and only the record separates expedient from earned."* The taxonomy was never written by the house, and the record alone does not separate the classes: timing, specificity, a test and a reader do. *Weakened; section 4 writes the definitions.*

*"The four-line override log … inherited verbatim [axiom]."* Printed five times in the house in three forms, and given a fourth form by this book's Chapters 2 to 5; the entry in section 11 has nine lines and is stated once. *Strengthened in intent, re-based in form.*

*The three build checks.* Re-based and kept, with a fourth. *Strengthened.*

*"Nine candidates … where this instrument would be first."* All nine have a version 2 home, each re-based: the "what this changed" provenance field is a line of three records and becomes one line of the one record (Chapters 9, 10, 11); the money map over the scene map is Chapter 10's money view, lit for a scene only after its governance has agreed, and Chapter 12's published method for the same thing; community-level control over being recommended is Chapter 10's consent and withdrawal tiers and Chapter 12's matching with content cues; the visibly ageing display is Chapter 8's degraded state with the last good copy and its age; the identity-and-conditions split with the crop test is Chapter 12's; the origination claim ladder is Chapter 6's rungs; the guess-as-perception-data moment is Chapter 9's self-placement with the private version run first; the "how this argues" page is Chapter 11's, Fixed as a stance there and Adaptable (house default) here; and the committee export at lower volume is Chapter 14's stripped state. None has been tested. Version 1's sentence stands: "If even half survive testing, the instrument is not a better dashboard — it is a new kind of thing"; in version 2's words, the nine are nine `[Prototype flag]`s and the register carries them so. *Strengthened.*

*The gap summary.* Version 1 listed eleven holes in the field; version 2 has 48 where the field has no study or no rule, with the same conclusion in the same place — the dashboard is where they are settled. *Strengthened.*

What the chapter found that the brief did not ask for: that the tier test's borrowed sentences needed checking (a year wrong; a question made an instruction); that the evidence on written reasons is thin and conditional; that the twelve records are one record and the three logs one log; and that Chapter 0 needs a revision that is a transcription of eleven decisions. *(Reading.)*

## Postscript, 7 October 2026 — what the register and the library pass showed

*Added after the chapter, and not part of its argument as written. The body above is unchanged. Research log, Entry 17.*

**The estimate of about two hundred Fixed rules is not confirmed.** Sections 3 and 8, the 60-second version and the list of things the chapter will not state as truths all carry an estimate made before the register existed: that re-basing would leave "perhaps two hundred" Fixed rules and about two hundred and fifty Adaptable. The register was built the next day (`06-register/rule-register.csv`; 377 rules from the 513 source rows, one per duplicate group, each row classified by this chapter's own rules with its reason recorded). It has 280 Fixed rules — 245 requirements and 35 sequences — which is 74 per cent, with 56 house defaults, 34 Adaptable and 7 Decorative, and one statement of the record listed apart. The re-basing did what section 3 said it would to the stances: 51 of the 90 became house defaults. What the estimate got wrong is the 212 unmarked Fixed rows and the 102 procedures, most of which proved to be presences, thresholds, lines of a record or prohibitions that a build can scan, recompute or check by date — requirements in this chapter's own sense. Section 3's first reading, which the chapter set aside ("dashboards are a subject of right answers, so the book's 89 per cent is the subject's"), is therefore nearer the truth than the chapter allowed, and the register's share is the Decision Book's own 74 per cent, not below it. What stands: the three senses; the retirement of "stance"; the eleven reconciliations; the entry; the one record and the one log. What is now open, and was the cold reader's objection to draft 1: whether a register of 280 Fixed rules, 245 of them build checks, is "short enough to be read", and whether hard stops at that number are obeyed or routed around. Truth 2 is the test of it, and the build's first year is where it is measured. `[Prototype flag]`

**The identifiers in section 8's ten first rows are illustrative.** The built register numbers its rules in its own order, so `DD-001` to `DD-010` there are not the register's `DD-001` to `DD-010`; the register's notes refer to those ten as "Chapter 16 §8 row n". The third ranking rule section 7 states — whether position is shown at all — has no source row of its own in any chapter and is carried in the register as the open part of the prohibition.

**Truth 2 rests on a tier that could not be disobeyed, more than its wording says.** Section 6 already notes that the tiered site in Paterno et al. 2009 used hard stops for its severest alerts. Read in full on 7 October, the paper says the hundred per cent was "by design since this was a 'hard stop'", and that the lowest tier cannot be compared; the evidence that setting rules apart raises compliance where people are free to override is the middle tier alone, 29 against 10 per cent. Truth 2's clause "the severe rules are obeyed only where they are set apart", and the rule in the table that cites it, should be read with that limit. This is one of ten corrections the library pass proposes across the book (`01-sources/LIBRARY-PASS.md`); none has been applied to any chapter, and each awaits Carly.

**The first entry in the deviation log exists.** `06-register/deviation-log.md` carries the nine-line spine, the two typed line sets, and DL-001 — Chapter 12's in-frame furniture against official practice — as *proposed*, with its test result pending the first build. The result printed in section 11's example ("18 of 20") was invented for method and is not in the log.
