# Working record · Chapter 5 — Building a measure

*How this chapter was made. These sections stood in the chapter at draft 2 and were moved here, word for word, on 14 October 2026 when the book was finished. They are the record of the method: what the chapter expected against what it found, what it did to the chapters before it, the gaps it met, where it met the design system and the Decision Book, and the loose ends it flagged. Nothing here is a rule; the rules are in the register, the open choices in the decision log, the probes in `03-tests/PROBES.md`.*

## Before you start

This is the fifth chapter of a research book written for Nightlife Culture, a creative policy studio led by Carly Heath, about how dashboards work and why. The book exists to support the Vibe Map — a planned public, non-commercial, interactive account of the people, activity, value and support systems that sustain a place's nightlife culture, built first for the West of England. You do not need to have read Chapters 1 to 4 to read this one. Four things from them recur: the working definition — *a dashboard is a display that turns data into a decision for a named reader*; Chapter 1's finding that the most reliable thing a published measure does is change the behaviour it measures; Chapter 2's finding that there is no reader in general; and Chapter 4's rule that a number is undefined until its unit, period and base are stated. Where this chapter refers to Carly, it means the studio's founder; "the brief" is her written brief for this book; where it refers to the Vibe Map, it is illustrating, not specifying. The studio's own rulebook for design decisions is called the Decision Book, and its visual rules live in a design system (Style Guide v1.5). One decision this chapter does not make and could not: whether the studio's measure of nightlife culture should be an index, a profile, a scoreboard, a ledger or something else. Version 1 of this book recorded that the studio has kept that question open on purpose; this chapter says what each form does and what each must show, and leaves the choice where it belongs.

Two conventions, explained fully in Chapter 0. Every rule in this book carries a **kind** — *Standard* (an external rule nobody here wrote), *Evidence* (a named study), *Reading* (our interpretation, open to challenge), *Practice* (a practitioner's framework or craft knowledge, not tested as research) or *Nightlife Culture* (a decision or finding of the studio's own) — and a **tier**: *Fixed* is a hard line with a right answer; *Adaptable* is a stated default you may argue with and must log if you leave; *Decorative* is free. "Fixed as a stance" marks a rule the studio adopts and will not reopen on a given display though its source is a reading or a practice; "Fixed as a procedure" fixes the steps and not the parameter. Flags in square brackets mark loose ends: `[Further research]`, `[Prototype flag]`, `[Validate with users]`, `[Revisit → Ch n]`, `[Revisit at end]`, `[Rich reference]` and `[Counterbalance → Ch n]`. A dagger (†) after a source means it was read at the authors' published abstract only; "secondary" means a quotation reached this chapter through another named document.

Three words are used in a fixed sense. A *construct* is the thing you want to measure and cannot see directly — deprivation, innovation, human development, the quality of a night out. An *indicator* is something you can count that stands for part of it. A *composite indicator*, or *index*, is what you get when several indicators are combined into one number. Most of this chapter is about the distance between the first and the third, and about what has to travel with the third for a reader to see that distance.

## Looking back

Against Chapters 0 to 4, the archived version 1, and the studio's Decision Book.

- **Strengthens** Chapter 1's truth that a published measure changes the behaviour it measures, with Campbell at source, Bevan and Hood's taxonomy and the audited figures — and **counterbalances** it: every rigorous comparison found the measured thing really improved, and three of the four ways a proxy fails need nobody to game anything. Chapter 1's truth should carry both halves. `[Counterbalance → Ch 1]`
- **Strengthens** Chapter 2's finding that the official reader needs the official currency (the Culture and Heritage Capital bank is that currency, built) and that advocates want a single number — while **opening a thread**: the composite's spread is described as advocacy-driven, and the sector's own research council prefers evaluation to measurement. The form question is partly the reader question.
- **Strengthens** Chapter 3's uncertainty rule (a rank carries its interval, countable, with a most-likely value) and its "show the shape" (a profile is addition's hidden imbalance made visible). **Strengthens** Chapter 3's precision rule with the ONS's "two effective digits" — Ehrenberg's rule is now official house style.
- **Strengthens** Chapter 4 throughout: a composite inherits every part's provenance; normalisation is a denominator choice; the funnel and the rank interval are one discipline; discriminant validity against platform coverage is Chapter 4's big-data paradox stated as a test. **Opens a thread** on "reliability", which now has three senses in the book.
- **Weakens** version 1's Chapter 6 in one respect and **strengthens** it in three. Version 1 cited the 20%/21% and 40%/81% figures without naming the index; this chapter finds them in Paruolo et al.'s Table 2 and they are the Times Higher Education ranking's, not the HDI's — the HDI is the well-behaved case in that paper. Version 1's "recipe on the face", its refusal test and its (21,1,1,1) illustration are confirmed at source or at the JRC's reproduction, and the sector composite's figures (8.58, 5.90, 5.41 against 6.10) are confirmed from the 2024 report — with version 1's inference corrected: the headline is not the mean of its parts, but several undisclosed recipes would produce it, so the finding is about disclosure and not arithmetic. Version 1's "intensity not volume" and "never merge identity with conditions" are the one-tailed and non-compensatory questions in this chapter's terms, and survive as Nightlife Culture rules for Chapter 6 and Chapter 9.
- **Opens a thread** for Chapter 0's glossary and legend: "reliability" in three senses; "validity" as of a reading for a use; "recognition" as face validity and credibility, which is neither nothing nor validity; and the fifth kind, *Practice*, used here for the belief that decision-makers prefer composites.
- **The Decision Book.** Chapters 6, 8 and 9 were searched for rules on composites, weights, aggregation and scores. **Silent on all of it.** Its breakout-statistic rule (a mandatory source line) and its precision rule (decimal places are a claim) apply to a composite's score and are consistent with this chapter; nothing in it governs how a score is built or what must accompany one. The rules table above is the candidate chapter. `[Counterbalance → Decision Book]`

## Design system (Style Guide v1.5) and Decision Book — conflict log

**Agrees.** "Every big number carries a source line. A statistic without one is a claim, not a finding" is, for a composed number, the card. "Illustrative or placeholder figures say so, in the chart, every time" covers a score published before its method is adopted: it is illustrative and must say so. "Every comparison between places declares whether the comparability gate was cleared" extends to a comparison of composite scores, which must additionally clear the aggregation gate — same recipe, same price list. "Strong design must never make weak evidence look stronger than it is" is, here, the rank shown without its interval and the score shown to two decimal places.

**Conflicts.** None direct. One tension: the design system's status chips and the Decision Book's tiers mark a claim's firmness, but neither has a component for the firmness of a *composed* number — the width of its rank interval, the gap between its nominal and effective weights. A "robustness" mark for a score, kin to the provenance code, would be the design system's part of truths 2 and 4. `[Counterbalance → design system]`

**Silent on.** Everything in sections 3 to 9 and 12: the ten steps, weights, aggregation, sensitivity, the audit, validity, recognition and the card. The Decision Book's data chapters stop at the chart and the table; the composite between the data and the chart has no chapter. This chapter's rules table is the candidate.
