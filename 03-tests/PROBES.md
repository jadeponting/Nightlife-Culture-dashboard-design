# Probe candidates
*Compiled 7 October 2026 from every `[Validate with users]` and `[Prototype flag]` line in Chapters 1–16 of version 2. Candidates, not a programme: prioritisation and the decision to run any of them are Carly's. Method only — nothing here is a finding about any place.*

## How to read this

A probe is a small, cheap test designed to settle one open question, with real people or with the build's own data. It asks one thing, of someone or something named, in a form that can be repeated, and its answer is set beside what was meant or expected. None of the probes below has been run.

The candidates come from two flags the chapters use. `[Validate with users]` marks something to test in conversation with the three user groups (policymakers and politicians; advocates and trade bodies; funders and investors) or with the scenes (artists, DJs, promoters, crews, venues). `[Prototype flag]` marks something the prototype can settle with its own data or its own build, which no literature can. Chapters 1 to 16 carry 189 such lines, numbered P-001 to P-189 in `register/work/probe-lines.md` with their chapter, line and section.

A probe takes one of five forms. In a reader test, someone named is shown something and asked a fixed question, and the answer is compared with what was meant. A count takes the build's own data or logs over a stated period. A comparison shows two versions with one variable changed and compares the pass rate. In a walk, someone with a stated intent tries to do something. A recomputation works a figure out again with one thing varied. Where a `[Prototype flag]` is about the build's own data the form is given as a count and what it needs is the build; "count, by recomputation" marks the ones that run a calculation again.

Each row of the table is one probe, and lines that ask the same test of different elements share a row. Rows sit under the part of the book where the question was first raised, in the book's order, which is not an order of importance; a line that names more than one test is listed under each and counted once (P-056, P-097, P-170, P-171, P-175). The sixth column names which of the Vibe Map's four tests the probe serves, in short: useful to all (can something universally useful be co-designed), recognised (does it paint a picture a scene recognises), steers (does it steer decisions and collective action), travels (can it travel to another place and stay useful). The seventh gives the measure and the pass in the chapter's wording where the line gives one; "the plainest one" marks a pass this register suggests because the line gives none, and "names no group" means the flag's own definition applies, the three user groups or the scenes. Cost is small (an afternoon with the data, or a question added to something already happening), a session (one sitting with readers) or a build (something has to be made, or kept running, before the test can be read).

## What the register shows

The 189 lines make 75 probes: 162 lines are placed and 27 are left out. Eleven probes name people from the scenes and sixteen name the three user groups without them; a further twenty-eight ask for readers, respondents or contributors without naming a group, and one needs ticket-holders. Nineteen the build can settle alone, from its own data, its logs or a test of its own machinery. The probe the most chapters depend on is the recognition check (probe 1), which six chapters ask for (Chapters 2, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 12); the use log and the kinds of nothing come next, with four chapters each. By cost, 27 are small, 36 need a session and 12 need a build first; which of them run, and in what order, is Carly's decision.
